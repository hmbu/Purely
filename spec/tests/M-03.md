# Tester report — M-03 "Order not sent" / "لم يتم إرسال الطلب"

Author: Tester (Agent 2)
Screen under test: `/spec/screens/M-03.md`
Read alongside: `/spec/screens-map.md`, `/spec/screens/G-03.md`, `/spec/screens/G-04.md`, `/spec/screens/M-01.md`
Special instruction carried into this walkthrough: the product manager has ruled that **the client order key must stay stable for the whole checkout session, replaced only when the server definitively says no order was created.** M-03's current text was written under the *old* rule (key replaced whenever any order value changes). Every place that text now conflicts with the new rule is flagged below.

---

## Persona 1 — Guest in a hurry at 2 AM, wants one product fast

Steps: adds one product, checks out, confirms room on M-01, connection blips, M-03 opens (connection variant).

- The modal itself adds no artificial friction for this persona: one tap on "Retry" (إعادة المحاولة) resends immediately, no counter, no cap, no forced wait beyond the request itself.
- Nothing in M-03's own behavior slows this guest down beyond what the connection failure already cost them. No finding specific to this persona beyond what is reported under Persona 6 below (the two personas converge on the same modal state).

## Persona 2 — Foreign guest who cannot read Arabic

Steps: guest scanned the QR without ever touching the language toggle (which lives only on G-01/G-08), browsed, checked out, hit a submission failure.

- M-03 has no language toggle of its own (confirmed by the file), and the spec states plainly "There is no path from M-03 to G-03, G-01, or any other screen" (§3.2). To reach the toggle, the guest must tap "Close" → G-04 → back/"Edit cart" → G-03 → back → G-01.
- Finding: at the exact moment the guest is trying to resolve a failed, possibly time-sensitive submission, they have no quick way to make the sheet legible; fixing the language costs three extra taps and two extra screens, undertaken in a language they cannot read. **Medium.**

## Persona 3 — Elderly guest with poor eyesight

Steps: same failure path; guest reads the sheet at arm's length.

- Text sizes on the sheet: C01 18 px bold, C02/C03 16 px, **C04 14 px** — the reception fallback line, which is the single most actionable instruction on the whole modal ("call reception... tell them your room number"), is set in the smallest text on the sheet (§7.4: "Nothing below 14 px").
- Finding: the most consequential line of copy is also the least prominent one, and it is not shown until the 3rd failed attempt, so it is invisible to this persona during the first two failures regardless of eyesight. **Medium.**
- No color-only signaling was found (fill/outline carries meaning per §7.4) — no finding there.

## Persona 4 — Guest who typed the wrong room number

Steps: guest suspects, while looking at M-03, that they may have mistyped the room number on G-04 before M-01 confirmed it.

- M-03 never shows the room number on the first or second failed attempt — `{room}` only appears inside C04, which is hidden until the 3rd consecutive failure (§4, §5.3 threshold). On attempts 1 and 2 there is nothing on the sheet the guest can check against their memory.
- The only way to verify what was actually submitted is to tap "Close" and look at G-04-F01 (which correctly keeps the value, per §3.2). This works, but it is indirect.
- Finding: a guest who doubts the room number they typed gets no on-sheet confirmation of it during the first two failures; they must leave the modal to check. **Minor** (the value is preserved and recoverable, just not visible here).

## Persona 5 — Guest whose product went out of stock before submitting

- This is explicitly not M-03's job. §5.4 correctly routes any stock-rejection response to M-04, and a stock-rejection whose product list is empty or unreadable is deliberately treated as a **server** M-03 failure rather than guessed at (§5.4 table, last exception noted correctly). Reported plainly: M-03's classification table draws this line correctly and no defect was found in it. No finding for this persona in this file (see the M-04 test file).

## Persona 6 — Guest with a weak connection that drops during submission (deepest walkthrough — this is the reason M-03 exists)

Step-by-step: guest on flaky hotel Wi-Fi taps "Confirm and send" on M-01. The request is sent, or fails to send, or is sent and the response never arrives. M-01 waits up to 15 s, then opens M-03 in the connection variant: C02 "لم نتلقَّ تأكيدًا بوصول طلبك إلى الفندق. / We have not received confirmation that your order reached the hotel." + C03 "تحقّق من اتصالك بالإنترنت ثم أعد المحاولة. إعادة المحاولة آمنة ولن تُرسل طلبك مرتين. / Check your internet connection, then try again. Retrying is safe and will never send your order twice."

Checks specifically requested:

**a) "It never tells the guest the order might already have gone through."** Confirmed — this is the deliberate design in §5.3. Walking through it as the persona: the copy is honest about what the device knows (no confirmation received) and gives one instruction (retry, and it's safe). Taken at face value this is enough to decide "what do I do right now" (tap Retry). But it is only actually true — i.e., retrying really is duplicate-safe — if nothing about the order changes between attempts, and that condition depends on client-order-key behavior the guest never sees. See the key-stability findings below: the text's safety claim is stronger than what the current key-replacement rule (as written elsewhere in this file and in G-04 §7.7) can actually guarantee once the guest touches "Close" and edits anything.

**b) No retry counter, no cap, no auto-retry; only the reception line changes, from the 3rd attempt.** Confirmed as written (§5.3, §4). Walking through repeated failures: on attempts 1 and 2 the guest has no way to know a fallback exists at all; they simply keep tapping the same button with the same copy. This is intentional per the spec's own reasoning, but from the guest's seat at 2 AM it means the first two failures give zero forward guidance beyond "try again" — they cannot tell, from the sheet, whether a third failure is expected to unlock something or whether they should already be considering the room phone. **Minor** (design is deliberate and the guidance does eventually appear).

**c) Retry in flight cannot be dismissed; backdrop and browser back are ignored.** Confirmed (§3.2, §6.1). Walking through it: each retry attempt can occupy up to 15 seconds during which B01, B02, the backdrop, and browser back all do nothing, and there is no progress indicator or countdown, so the guest cannot tell whether 1 second or 14 seconds remain. On a genuinely weak/dropping connection, several consecutive attempts each locking the guest in for up to 15 seconds, with no way out, is a real source of anxiety and perceived unresponsiveness, even though it is safety-motivated. **Medium.**

**d) Connection failure and server error get different copy.** Confirmed — verified the two C03 variants in §5.2 are textually distinct and match the classification table in §5.4. No defect found here. One phrasing note: the connection-variant instruction "Check your internet connection, then try again" presumes an ongoing, checkable problem; it reads oddly for the specific persona named here — a connection that *drops* and may already have recovered by the time the sheet is read — since there may be nothing left for the guest to "check." **Minor.**

**e) The §5.5 "accepted limitation" and the new key-stability rule.** This is the headline problem in this file. §5.5 point 4 currently reads (quoted in full because the exact wording is the finding):

> "Accepted limitation, stated so it is not mistaken for an oversight: if a response was lost after the server created the order, and the guest then closes M-03, changes the order, and submits the new version, the server receives a new key and creates a second order. Version 1 accepts this: the guest was told no confirmation was received and that Retry is safe; the hotel receives both orders with the same room number and staff resolve it at the door. No device-side detection of this case exists in version 1."

Under the product manager's new rule — key stable for the whole checkout session, replaced only when the server *definitively* says no order was created — this exact scenario is precisely what the new rule is designed to prevent, because a lost/ambiguous response is **not** a definitive "no order created" signal. So:

- §5.5 point 4 is now describing a bug the product owner has decided must not happen, not an accepted limitation. As written, it still tells the technical team to accept it.
- §5.5 point 3 says: "If the guest changes any order value first, G-04 replaces the key (G-04 §7.7) and resets the attempt count." This is the exact old-rule behavior that produces the scenario in point 4. Under the new rule, an order edit following an *ambiguous* M-03 failure (as opposed to a *definitive* M-04 stock rejection) should not, by itself, replace the key — but the text still says it does, and cites G-04 §7.7 as if that were still correct.
- §3.1's closing sentence — "the guest then taps 'Submit order' → M-01 → 'Confirm and send', which reuses the same key **because nothing changed**" — frames key-reuse as conditional on the order being unchanged, which is the old-rule framing the new ruling replaces.
- Acceptance criterion 14 in §8 states as a pass/fail test: "After 'Close', changing any order value... and submitting again sends a new client order key, and the attempt count for M-03 starts again at 1 on the next failure." This criterion now encodes the *old, superseded* rule as something a build must satisfy to pass.

**Rated Critical** — the text a technical team would build from still specifies the exact key-replacement behavior the product manager has since overturned, in four places in this one file.

**f) A related gap the new rule opens, that no part of this file addresses.** If the key is meant to stay the same across an order edit that follows an ambiguous failure, the spec nowhere describes what should happen when a request carrying a *previously seen key* now carries a **different payload** than the attempt the server may or may not have already processed. §5.5 point 1 ("Retry resends the byte-identical payload... with the same client order key") only covers the case where payload and key move together; it has no answer for "same key, changed payload," which is exactly the case the new rule creates the moment a guest edits the order after Close instead of tapping Retry unmodified. Without a stated rule here, the risk is that the wrong items or the wrong room number reach staff, or that the guest's edited order is silently discarded as a "duplicate" of an order that in fact never went through. **Rated Critical** — this is squarely "an operational error reaches the staff" territory, and it stems directly from the same file's now-outdated key-stability text.

**g) Is what the guest is told enough to decide what to do, in a hotel room at night with a dropping connection?** For the immediate decision ("tap Retry now"), yes — the copy is clear and actionable. For the deeper question the guest is implicitly relying on the modal to answer honestly — "if I go back and fix something first, is it still safe?" — the modal's own promise ("Retrying is safe and will never send your order twice") is stated without qualification, but the guest-facing text elsewhere in this same file (§5.5 point 3, §3.1, acceptance criterion 14) still assumes editing the order changes the key, which is the very thing that could reintroduce the duplicate the modal just promised won't happen. The guest is not told, and cannot be told from this text, that "safe to retry unmodified" and "safe to edit and resend" are not (as currently written) the same guarantee.

## Persona 7 — Guest who wants to change the order after submitting it

- Not applicable to M-03: this modal only exists before a success response is received (§1, §6.4); once an order is confirmed the guest is on G-05/G-06, outside M-03's scope entirely. Within M-03 itself, "Close" correctly returns to G-04 so the guest can edit before a first successful send — that is a *pre*-submission change, not the "after submitting" case this persona describes. No finding here; reported plainly as out of scope for this modal.
