# Tester report — M-04 "Items no longer available" / "منتجات غير متوفرة"

Author: Tester (Agent 2)
Screen under test: `/spec/screens/M-04.md`
Read alongside: `/spec/screens-map.md`, `/spec/screens/G-03.md`, `/spec/screens/G-04.md`, `/spec/screens/M-01.md`, `/spec/tests/M-03.md`

## Persona 1 — Guest in a hurry at 2 AM, wants one product fast

Steps: adds exactly one product, checks out, confirms on M-01, the product turns out to have sold out in the meantime → M-04 opens.

- With a single-item cart, a rejection always falls in the **All** case (§5.2): C01 "نفد كل ما في طلبك / Everything in your order has sold out", B01 "إزالته من السلة / Remove it from the cart".
- Tapping B01 removes the line and lands on G-03's empty state (§3.2), from which the guest still needs to tap "متابعة التسوق / Continue shopping" to reach G-01 before they can pick something else.
- Finding: for exactly the persona the "one product fast" case describes, the fastest legitimate path from "sold out" back to "browsing again" is still two taps and two screens (M-04 → G-03 empty state → G-01), each of them doing real, necessary work (delete line, then leave the cart). Not a defect in what the screens do, but worth naming: nothing on M-04 itself shortens this for the single-item case. **Minor.**

## Persona 2 — Foreign guest who cannot read Arabic

Steps: same as the M-03 walkthrough — guest never touched the language toggle, reaches M-04 in a language they cannot read.

- Same structural gap as M-03: M-04 has no language toggle. The only way out is B01 (→ G-04 or G-03 empty state, both reachable to G-01) or B02 (→ G-03 with the affected lines out of stock, then back to the original opener, eventually G-01).
- Finding: as with M-03, resolving the language problem is not possible from within the modal and requires navigating through at least one more screen before the toggle is reachable, while the guest is trying to understand which of their items were just rejected. **Medium.**

## Persona 3 — Elderly guest with poor eyesight

Steps: same failure path; guest reads the sheet.

- Text sizes here are all 16 px or larger (§7.6: "Nothing below 16 px on this modal") — no text-size finding, unlike M-03.
- The rejected-item rows are the one place where size is not the limiting factor: each row is "{qty} × {name}" only, no image (§5.3). For an elderly guest who may rely on recognizing a product's photo more than its printed name (especially for products whose English name is substituted with the untranslated Arabic name, per §5.3), a text-only row is harder to positively identify than the same line would be with its 64 px thumbnail from G-03. See Persona 5 below for the full treatment of this; noted here as it applies with extra force to this persona. **Medium** (folded into the Persona 5 finding below to avoid double-counting).

## Persona 4 — Guest who typed the wrong room number

- Not applicable: M-04 never displays, reads, or changes the room number (§5.9: "no room number" is explicitly excluded from the sheet). Reported plainly: no finding, correctly out of scope.

## Persona 5 — Guest who added a product that went out of stock before submitting (deepest walkthrough — this is the reason M-04 exists)

Step-by-step: guest adds several products on G-01/G-02 (recognizing them mainly by photo and price while browsing), reviews the cart on G-03 (availability check there reported everything available), checks out, confirms the room number on M-01. Between the G-03 check and the server processing the submission, one or more items sell out. The server returns a stock rejection; M-01 hands off to M-04.

**a) "Rows show quantity and name only — no image, no price, no per-row control. Can the guest recognise what they are losing?"**

Walking through it: the row reads e.g. "2 × قهوة مثلجة" / "2 × Iced coffee". The guest who added it a few minutes earlier while scrolling a grid of product photos now must match that photo-memory to a bare product name, with:
- No image to compare against.
- No price, which was often part of how the guest distinguished near-duplicate items (e.g., two sizes or two flavors of the same product line).
- No per-row control, so there's nothing to tap to "check" — the guest can only read the name and decide, in aggregate, whether to accept the whole batch removal or go back to the cart.

This is a real recognition gap for the exact case the modal exists to handle. It doesn't cause an operational error (removal is done by product identifier, not by the guest's understanding — so nothing wrong is ever sent to staff because of this), but it can leave the guest uncertain about what they are actually agreeing to lose, especially when several rows are listed at once or when a name is shown in the "wrong" language (§5.3: "if the English name is missing, the Arabic name is shown in the English interface too" — an English-reading guest may not recognize an unfamiliar Arabic string at all). **Medium.**

**b) "Three copy cases (one item, several, everything) and whether the all-sold-out case ever reads as 'some'."**

Checked directly against §5.2, §4, and acceptance criterion 5: the "All" case copy ("نفد كل ما في طلبك / Everything in your order has sold out" and the matching C02/B01 text) never uses "some" / "بعض" or "the rest" / "باقي" — the spec explicitly calls this out and requires it. Walking a guest with a single rejected line that also happens to be their only cart line: this correctly resolves to "All, N = 1" rather than "Some, N = 1" (the "All" condition is "every cart line is rejected," §5.2), so the boundary case is handled correctly. **No finding** — this was checked specifically per the task and no defect was found.

**c) "The new total appears under the list except in the all case."**

Confirmed present in both "Some" cases and absent (and correctly so, since it would read "0.00") in the "All" case (§5.4). One phrasing note: the label "الإجمالي بعد الإزالة / Total after removal" describes a state as if the removal has already happened, when in fact nothing is removed until B01 is tapped — the number is a preview of a consequence, not a completed fact. A guest skimming quickly could read this as "what I now owe" rather than "what I would owe if I proceed." **Minor.**

**d) "Marks are written when the modal opens, not when the button is tapped, so a reload still shows the products out of stock."**

Walked through directly: §3.1 states the marks are written "on opening, before any tap," and §3.1's reload paragraph confirms that a reload while M-04 is open lands the guest on G-03 (not G-04, because G-04's render guard sees the marks and defers to G-03) with the affected lines already shown out of stock. This was checked specifically and works as intended: the guest cannot lose the "this item is unavailable" information by refreshing, and nothing is silently re-added to a submittable cart. **No finding** — behaves as required.

**e) "In the all-sold-out case both buttons lead to G-03 by different routes."**

Walked through both: in the "All" case, B01 ("إزالته/إزالتها من السلة / Remove it/them from the cart") deletes every line in one write and lands on G-03's **empty** state. B02 ("العودة إلى السلة / Back to cart") leaves the cart untouched and lands on G-03 with every one of those same lines shown in their **out-of-stock form**, each requiring its own "إزالة / Remove" tap — G-03 explicitly has no bulk-remove control (G-03 §5.4: "There is no 'remove all unavailable' control on G-03 in version 1; with several such lines the guest taps 'Remove' on each"). Since in the "All" case there is, by definition, nothing left in the cart that isn't already listed on M-04, choosing B02 over B01 gains the guest no additional information and costs them one manual removal tap per line to reach the exact same empty cart that B01 would have produced immediately. Finding: the two exits are not equivalent in effort even though nothing is gained by taking the costlier one, and the modal gives no signal that this is the case. **Medium.**

**f) "A second rejection for a different item reopens the modal with no special copy — walk a guest hitting this twice in a row."**

Walked through: guest hits M-04 for item A (Some, N=1), taps B01, returns to G-04, resubmits, and a *different* item (item B) is now rejected. Per §5.8 (explicitly by design), M-04 reopens with the exact same generic "Some, N=1" copy, no mention that this is a second rejection, no running count of how many items have now been dropped from the original order, and no acknowledgment of the pattern. From the guest's seat: two failures of the same kind in quick succession, worded identically both times, with no reassurance and no visible sign the system recognizes it has now happened twice. For a tired guest this reads as the store being unreliable rather than as two independent, unlucky stock events. **Medium.**

A second, related effect not mentioned in M-04's own text but caused by it: each pass through M-04 replaces the client order key and **resets M-03's attempt count to 0** (§5.6 step 3, §5.7). Walking through a guest who alternates between stock rejections and connection failures (a plausible combination on a busy night with weak Wi-Fi): every time a stock rejection occurs, any progress toward M-03's "3rd failed attempt" reception-fallback line is wiped out, so a guest could accumulate well more than three total failed attempts across the session before ever seeing that fallback guidance, because stock rejections keep resetting the counter that was supposed to catch a guest who is struggling. **Medium.**

**g) "What the guest understands about whether their order was sent."**

Walked through the exact wording: C02's "Some" copy reads "This item sold out before your order was sent. Remove it, then send the rest of your order." / "نفد هذا المنتج قبل إرسال طلبك. أزله ثم أرسل باقي الطلب." This is worded correctly if read carefully (the stockout happened *before* sending could complete, and sending is still to be done), but unlike M-03 — which states outright "we have not received confirmation that your order reached the hotel" — M-04 never makes an equally direct statement such as "no order has been created / nothing has reached the hotel yet." The guest must infer this from the indirect past-tense phrasing and from the fact that action buttons are being offered at all. Finding: a guest reading quickly, or reading in their second language, could plausibly misread "before your order was sent" as meaning the order already went out (minus the unavailable items) rather than that nothing has been sent at all. **Medium.**

## Persona 6 — Guest with a weak connection that drops during submission

- Not applicable in the way it is for M-03: a stock rejection is, by definition, a response the server actually returned (§5.7: "a stock rejection is a definite server response"), so reaching M-04 already implies the connection worked well enough to deliver that response. §3.1 correctly routes a rejection with an empty or unreadable product list to M-03's server variant instead of opening M-04 with nothing to show — this boundary was checked and is handled correctly. No finding for this persona in this file; reported plainly as out of scope for M-04 (see the M-03 test file for the connection-drop walkthrough).

## Persona 7 — Guest who wants to change the order after submitting it

- Not applicable: M-04 only ever appears before an order exists (a stock rejection means no order was created, §5.7). "Change the order after submitting" is G-06/M-02 territory, outside M-04 entirely. No finding; reported plainly as out of scope.
