# Tester report — M-01 Confirm room number (تأكيد رقم الغرفة)

Tester: Agent 2
Screens read: `/home/user/Purely/CLAUDE.md`, `/home/user/Purely/spec/screens-map.md`, `/home/user/Purely/spec/screens/G-04.md`, `/home/user/Purely/spec/screens/M-01.md`.

Scope note: M-01 is tested as **the single guard between a mistyped room number and a delivery to the wrong room** (G-04 §5.3 explicitly declines to check the room number against any real room list). Findings below are rated only on what M-01 itself does or fails to do. Where a persona's real trouble lives on another screen (G-01/G-03/G-04/M-03/M-04/G-06), that is stated plainly instead of inventing an M-01 fault.

Severity scale: **Critical** = guest cannot complete the journey, or an operational error reaches staff. **Medium** = guest completes the journey but is confused, slowed, or likely to make a mistake. **Minor** = polish.

## Persona 1 — Guest in a hurry at 2 AM, wants one product fast

Walkthrough: taps "Submit order" on G-04, M-01 fades in within 100 ms, the room number is huge and legible at a glance, one dark primary button sits exactly where the thumb already is. This is the fastest possible confirmation surface — no typing, no scrolling in the ready state.

- **[Minor] F-1.1 — Arming delay gives no feedback.** For 300 ms after open, taps on B01/B02 are silently swallowed, and the spec is explicit that "the buttons look active from the first frame" with no disabled styling during that window (§7.2). A guest who taps immediately (very plausible at 2 AM, mid-motion from tapping "Submit order") gets zero indication the first tap did anything. It resolves itself half a second later, but in the moment it can read as "the button didn't register" and invite a confused extra tap.
- **[Medium] F-1.2 — Nothing here can catch a payment-method or item mistake made one screen ago.** M-01 deliberately shows only the room number (§5.4, by design). A hurried guest who skimmed G-04 and picked the wrong payment option, or didn't notice a wrong quantity, gets no second look at any of that here — the one mandatory stop in the whole flow confirms only one of the several facts staff need. Not wrong-room-critical, but a plausible delivery-time mismatch (wrong payment method expected) that nothing downstream of G-04 catches. See also the general note under "Explicit checks" below.

No missing button or unclear state was found for this persona beyond the two items above; the core interaction (read number, tap once) is as fast as this kind of gate can be.

## Persona 2 — Foreign guest who cannot read Arabic

M-01 has no language toggle of its own (by design — only G-01/G-08 carry it) and inherits whatever language is saved on the device. If this guest reached checkout without ever finding G-01's toggle, everything on M-01 — title, the "your order will be delivered to room" sentence, and both button labels — is in a language they cannot read.

- **[Medium] F-2.1 — Two unreadable, icon-less buttons, with no escape to fix the language.** B01 and B02 carry no icons (§5.3 explicitly: "Neither button contains ... any icon"), only text. Because the modal is non-dismissable and browser back is ignored, a guest stuck on the wrong language cannot leave M-01 to go find the toggle on G-01 — they must guess which of two text-only buttons to press, relying only on visual weight (filled vs. outlined) as a convention that is not universal. The consequence of a wrong guess is recoverable in both directions here (confirming is what they came to do; editing just loops back to G-04), which keeps this from being Critical, but it is a real point of confusion at the one screen the product is most relying on the guest to act correctly on.
- The **digits themselves are unaffected** — Western digits 0–9 are used in both languages (confirmed in §5.2) — so at least the number the guest is confirming is legible to them regardless of language. This part works correctly; no fault found there.

## Persona 3 — Elderly guest with poor eyesight

This is where M-01's design choices are put under the most direct pressure. Walking through carefully:

- The room number at 64 px bold, 3.5× the next-largest text, high-contrast (≥7:1) box: this is a strong, well-justified choice and reads correctly for this persona. No fault found in the core legibility of C03.
- Pinch-zoom and text scaling are explicitly never disabled (§7.6), and the card is stated to grow and scroll internally if content doesn't fit (§5.1 clearance rule). Good coverage for a guest who relies on browser/OS zoom.
- **[Medium] F-3.1 — Fixed 112 px box height is not confirmed to scale with OS/browser text-size boosting.** §5.1/§7.6 guarantee the *card* grows and scrolls when content is enlarged, and that the number "never shrinks below 64 px," but C03's box is specified as a fixed "112 px tall" container (§4, §5.2). Pinch/viewport zoom scales everything uniformly and is fine, but a separate accessibility feature many low-vision users rely on — OS- or browser-level font-size boosting that enlarges text without scaling containers — is not addressed. If the digits render larger than the 64 px baseline under that kind of setting, the spec does not say whether the 112 px box grows with them or the digits clip/overflow it.
- **[Minor] F-3.2 — Disabled B02 in the loading state is signaled only by 50% opacity.** §5.2 states nothing is signaled by color alone, and 50% opacity is arguably a luminance/contrast change rather than "color," but for a guest with genuinely poor eyesight a subtle opacity shift on an already-outlined (low-weight) button may not be perceptible. The consequence of tapping it anyway is harmless (it is ignored), so this stays Minor rather than Medium.
- **[Minor] F-3.3 — B02's outlined style may read as lower "tappability" than B01's filled style.** The spec fixes size (310×56) and a 12 px gap, but does not specify a minimum border weight/contrast for the outline itself, distinct from the generic 1 px borders used elsewhere for static, non-interactive boxes (e.g., C03). A low-vision guest scanning quickly could plausibly perceive only one of the two rows as an actionable button.

## Persona 4 — Guest who typed the wrong room number

This is the persona the whole modal exists for. Two branches were walked.

**Branch A — the guest notices the mistake on M-01.**

They tap "Edit room number" (B02): M-01 closes immediately, G-04 shows F01 focused, keypad open, cursor at the end of the existing (wrong) value — nothing is pre-selected (§3.2, confirmed by G-04 §3.1).

- **[Medium] F-4.1 — No select-all on refocus makes the correction itself error-prone.** Because the cursor lands at the end of the wrong value with nothing selected, the guest must manually delete the old digits before typing the right ones. On the single field this whole modal exists to protect, a partial delete (e.g., they mean to fix "305"→"350" but only backspace one digit and end up with "3505," which is still a valid-length, plausible-looking number) produces a *new*, still-wrong number that only the same fallible "read and compare" step at the next M-01 open can catch. Given the stated purpose of this modal, making the edit path itself a source of a fresh typo is a real weakness, not just polish.
- They resubmit, a fresh M-01 opens with the corrected number. No request was ever sent for the wrong number in this branch, so there is no server-side residue to worry about here — this specific path is safe as written.

**Branch B — the guest does NOT notice the mistake and confirms it.**

- **[Critical] F-4.2 — The guard's entire effectiveness rests on the guest actively comparing digits, and nothing on M-01 enforces that comparison.** Large type and a flat 300 ms delay make the number easy to read *if looked at*, but they do nothing for the case size cannot help with: a guest who mistyped their own remembered number (e.g., transposed "305" into "350," or misremembers which room they're in) will see exactly the digits they expect to see, because the error is in their head, not on the screen. There is no re-typing, no matching against something independent, no forced pause proportional to the number's length — just a label, a big number, and a button that is live after 300 ms regardless of whether the guest looked up. Since G-04 explicitly forgoes any server-side room-list check (G-04 §5.3), this passive read-and-tap is the *only* mechanism in the entire product standing between a mistyped number and staff arriving at the wrong door. That qualifies as Critical under "an operational error reaches the staff."
- Once confirmed, M-01's job in this branch is done correctly per spec (it sends exactly what it was handed, byte-for-byte, §7.1) — the fix for this branch lives entirely outside M-01, in cancel-before-acceptance (M-02) and reorder, which is out of scope for this file and is not re-tested here.

**General observation for this persona:** a guest who, upon seeing the number on M-01, decides they don't want to submit *at all* right now (not "wrong number," but "I changed my mind") has only "Edit room number" to escape through — see "Explicit checks" below for the non-dismissable walkthrough.

## Persona 5 — Guest who added a product that went out of stock before submitting

M-01 correctly delegates this scenario. By the time M-01 opens, G-04's render guard has already ensured no cart line is marked out-of-stock in the *session catalog copy* (G-04 §3.1); M-01 takes an immutable snapshot at open (§3.1 "Snapshot rule") and never re-reads the cart. If a line goes out of stock on the server *during* the confirm request, M-01 correctly and transparently routes that to M-04 as "Outcome 3" in the same frame, without exposing G-04 in between (§3.2, §7.5). No fault was found in M-01 itself for this persona — the mechanism it relies on (M-04) is outside this file's scope. One neutral observation: while the request is in flight the guest cannot tell whether the eventual outcome will be success, a connection failure, or a stock rejection — the "Sending…" label is generic by design (§6.1) — which is consistent and not a defect.

## Persona 6 — Guest with a weak connection that drops during submission

This branch was walked in depth, including the wall-clock timeout and backgrounding rules.

- **Backgrounding / device sleep is well specified.** §3.3 correctly ties the 15-second timeout to wall-clock time from send, not a pausable timer, and correctly handles resume in all three sub-cases (response arrived / ≥15 s elapsed / <15 s elapsed). No fault found in this specific mechanism as written.
- **[Medium] F-6.1 — Fifteen seconds with zero progress feedback is a hard wait for exactly the guest most likely to distrust it.** No elapsed-time cue, no percentage, no "still trying" text exists during loading (§5.4, §6.1 — deliberately deferred to backlog per the file's own note). A guest already experiencing a weak connection is the one most likely to interpret an unmoving spinner as a frozen page, and is the most likely to act on that belief (see F-6.2).
- **[Critical] F-6.2 — Closing the tab (not just backgrounding it) during an in-flight request is not covered, and the guest's own device-side safety net (the client order key) is destroyed at exactly that moment.** §3.3 specifies "reload" and "tab backgrounded / discarded-and-restored" as the two interruption cases, but a guest who force-closes the tab or browser outright (a very plausible reaction to what F-6.1 describes as looking like a freeze) is a third case that is not addressed here. G-04 §7.7 states the checkout draft — including the client order key — "is discarded... when the browser tab is closed," and that "a new tab starts with an empty draft." If the in-flight request had already reached the server before the tab closed (a realistic outcome on a *weak but not dead* connection, where the request goes out but the response never returns), the server may create a real order for the wrong or right room, the device never learns of it (no order record is ever written — that only happens on step 1 of §7.4, which requires the page to still be running to receive the response), and the guest — now on a fresh tab with an untouched cart and no memory of any of this — can easily re-add the same items and submit again under a brand-new key, producing a genuine duplicate order that staff must sort out. This is exactly the "operational error reaches staff" case, and the one interruption path most associated with a weak connection is the one path the file does not walk through.
- **[Minor] F-6.3 — Repeated weak-connection cycles compound the silent wait.** Not a fault of M-01 as such (the retry loop lives in M-03), but worth noting: each M-03 "Retry" re-enters an M-01 instance with the same unaddressed 15-second silent wait (F-6.1), so a persistently weak connection means the guest experiences that same feedback-free wait multiple times in a row before any explicit choice is offered again.

## Persona 7 — Guest who wants to change the order after submitting it

By the time this persona's need arises, M-01 has already closed and torn down its own state: on success it discards the draft and the client order key and hands off to G-05 (§7.4 steps 3–5). M-01 has no post-submission role, no editing surface, and nothing in its spec claims otherwise — this is correctly and explicitly out of scope for this modal (§5.4: "no inline editing of the number... a guest who wants to change anything other than the room number taps B02, which lands on G-04"). No fault found in M-01 for this persona; the actual mechanism for this need (cancel while status is New via M-02, then reorder) lives entirely outside this file and was not re-tested here.

## Explicit checks requested

**Nothing shown but the room number (§5.4).** Confirmed: the guest confirms blind to total, payment method, item count, and notes. See F-1.2 (Medium) — this is a deliberate, documented trade-off, but it is also a real gap: the one mandatory checkpoint in the flow cannot catch anything except a wrong room number, even though it is the guest's last chance to catch anything at all before sending.

**Non-dismissable modal — accidental open / guest changes their mind about ordering.** Walked both cases:
- Accidental open (e.g., a mis-tap on G-04's "Submit order" while scrolling): the guest can still get out via "Edit room number," which functionally acts as a universal "never mind, don't send" button even though it is labeled and intended as a room-number edit action.
- Guest changes their mind about the *entire order* while looking at M-01: there is no action for this. They must tap "Edit room number" (landing back on a still-populated G-04), and only then use G-04's own back arrow or "Edit cart" to actually leave. **[Medium] F-7.1 — A guest who wants to abandon the order entirely at the one screen designed to make them pause and think has no direct way to do so; they must take an action labeled for a different purpose, then two more taps on the screen underneath.** This is consistent with the "one job, no third action" rationale in §5.4, but it does mean the modal's own stated goal — a genuine pause for reconsideration — has no exit for the specific outcome "I reconsidered and no longer want to order."

**Arming delay (300 ms) + 96 px clearance vs. double-tap on G-04's Submit.** Verified against the numbers in both files: G-04's fixed bottom bar (S06) occupies the literal bottom 88 px of the viewport, where B05 sits; M-01's clearance rule guarantees the card's bottom edge — and therefore B01, the last element inside it — is never closer than 96 px to the viewport bottom, in both the normal and the short-viewport fallback layouts (§5.1). A second tap at B05's former screen position can therefore only land on G-04's own 300 ms debounce or on M-01's inert backdrop, never on M-01's buttons. No fault found in this mechanism; it is sound as specified.

**Never returns to ready; no cancel-sending; no progress cue for up to 15 s.** Confirmed as designed (§6.1, §7.3 rule 6). See F-6.1 (Medium, the feedback gap itself) and F-6.2 (Critical, the tab-closure consequence of that gap going unaddressed).

**15-second timeout is wall-clock.** Confirmed correctly specified for backgrounding/locking/discard-and-reload (§3.3). No fault found there; the gap is specifically the tab-closure case (F-6.2), which is a different interruption than backgrounding.

**"Edit room number" behavior.** Confirmed it closes the modal and focuses G-04's F01 with the cursor at the end of the existing value, nothing sent, draft and key untouched (§3.2). See F-4.1 (Medium) for the lack of select-all making the correction itself error-prone.

**Success sequence (save → empty cart → discard draft → replace history → show G-05, within 100 ms).** Confirmed the ordering is deliberately crash-safe: the order record is written to the device in step 1, before the cart is touched or the draft is discarded, so an interruption anywhere after step 1 still leaves the order recoverable via G-07. No fault found in this sequence as written.

**Accessibility (digit-by-digit reading, ≥7:1 contrast, no color-only signaling, 16 px minimum text).** Largely well specified (§5.2, §7.6). See F-3.1 (Medium, box height vs. OS text-boosting) and F-3.2/F-3.3 (Minor, disabled-state and outline-weight perceptibility) for the specific gaps found.

**Client order key used exactly as handed; M-01 never changes it.**

Confirmed: M-01's own text is internally consistent — it never creates, changes, or discards the key (§7.1, §7.3 layer 4, §8 criterion 15). The problem is not what M-01 does with the key; it is that **M-01's description of when the key changes (inherited from G-04 §7.7) is written under the rule the product manager has since replaced.**

- **[Critical] F-KEY-1 — M-01's text follows the old "replace key on any change" rule; under the new "stable key for the whole checkout session" rule, the server-matching behavior M-01 itself relies on becomes unsafe.** Specifically:
  - Current text (§7.3 layer 4, "Changed order" bullet): editing the room number (or notes, payment method, amount, or cart) after M-01 has opened once causes G-04 to hand M-01 a **new** key on the next submission, "so the changed order is a new order and can never be swallowed as a duplicate."
  - Current text (§7.3 layer 5, server rule M-01 depends on): "a submission whose client order key has already produced an order must return that existing order as a success... and must not create another."
  - Under the **stable-key rule**, the first bullet no longer holds: a guest who sees an ambiguous failure (M-03, from a dropped connection or timeout — persona 6) where the request may have *actually succeeded* server-side, returns to G-04, corrects something that matters (most importantly: the room number, per persona 4) and resubmits, would now carry the **same** key as the earlier, possibly-already-fulfilled attempt. Combined with the still-current server rule quoted above, the server would treat the corrected submission as a duplicate of the earlier one, discard the guest's correction, and return the **original (uncorrected, possibly wrong-room) order** as "success." M-01 would then run its normal success sequence (§7.4) and show G-05 as if the guest's corrected order had gone through — while the order actually on file is the one with the mistake the guest thought they had just fixed. This is precisely the failure mode M-01 exists to prevent, produced by the interaction of the stable-key rule with wording M-01 has not yet been updated for.
  - Concrete spots in this file that state the now-outdated behavior and would need reconciling (reported, not fixed, per scope): the "Changed order" bullet in §7.3 layer 4; the general "Client order key" paragraph carried over from G-04 §7.7 quoted in §7.3's lead-in; acceptance criterion 25 ("...after B02 and a change to the room number, the new M-01 instance carries a different key").
  - Note: the "Same order, sent again" bullet in §7.3 layer 4 (identical retried payload keeps the same key) is unaffected by the rule change and remains correct either way.
