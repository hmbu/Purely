# Tester report — M-02 (Cancel order? / إلغاء الطلب؟)

Author: Tester (Agent 2)
Screen under test: `/spec/screens/M-02.md`
Context read: `/CLAUDE.md`, `/spec/screens-map.md`, `/spec/screens/G-06.md`

Reminder for the reader: this file reports problems only. It does not propose solutions and no spec file was edited.

M-02 is the only repair path in the whole product for a wrong room number, a wrong item, or a change of mind (locked decision 6). Personas 4 and 7 arrive here directly and are treated as decisive; persona 6 is treated as decisive because a cancellation races staff acceptance while the request is in flight. For personas whose trouble is created elsewhere in the product, this report states only what M-02 itself does or fails to do.

---

## Persona 1 — Guest in a hurry at 2 AM who wants one product fast

This persona rarely needs to cancel, so the walkthrough assumes they realize a mistake right after submitting and want to fix it fast.

Steps: G-06 (status New) → tap "إلغاء الطلب / Cancel order" → M-02 slides up → guest, still moving fast, taps the bottom button on reflex before reading it.

- The bottom button, at the position a thumb naturally rests after tapping a button in the same screen region, is "الاحتفاظ بالطلب / Keep order" — the dark-filled primary. "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel," the action the guest actually wants, is the outlined button *above* it. A guest who taps by position rather than by reading lands on "Keep order," not on the cancel they came for.
- The failure mode is safe (the order is not touched), but nothing on M-02 or G-06 afterward tells the guest "you just tapped Keep order, your order is still active." The sheet simply closes and G-06 looks like it did before the tap. A hurried guest who does not notice the sheet closed without effect may believe the cancellation happened and stop paying attention. **[Medium — F1]**
- The 300 ms arming delay (both buttons ignore taps with no visual reaction at all) means a guest who taps the instant the sheet appears gets no feedback and may tap again, believing the first tap did not register; this matches the M-01 convention already accepted elsewhere in the product, so it is noted for completeness only and not rated as a new finding.

## Persona 2 — Foreign guest who cannot read Arabic

By the time a guest reaches G-06/M-02 they have necessarily passed through G-01, where the only language toggle in the product lives, so in the ordinary path the correct language is already selected before checkout ever happens.

- M-02 has no language toggle of its own (matching G-06). If, for any reason, the device's saved language is not the guest's language by the time they reach this modal, there is no way to switch without abandoning the flow, going back to G-01, toggling, and returning. This risk is low in practice but the escape hatch does not exist on this screen. **[Minor — F2]**
- Everything else on M-02 (order number, layout) is language-neutral or numeric, so this persona has no other friction specific to M-02 itself.

## Persona 3 — Elderly guest with poor eyesight

Steps: same as persona 1, but reading carefully rather than moving fast.

- Text sizes (18 px title, 16 px body/buttons, 14 px reception line) and stated contrast minimums (4.5:1) meet the product's own floor, and browser zoom/text scaling are not disabled, which mitigates the base legibility risk.
- However, the two buttons are told apart only by fill-vs-outline and by short text labels — there is no icon on the sheet in any state. A filled button visually reads as "the more important / default one" in most mobile-UI conventions, which is exactly "Keep order" here, not the cancel the guest is looking for. A guest with reduced visual acuity who scans for the visually stronger target before reading both labels carefully is nudged toward the button that does not perform the action they came to do. Both buttons are the same size, so this is a weight-of-fill issue, not a size issue. **[Medium — F3]**
- Locating "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" therefore depends entirely on reading a short label rendered in a visually secondary (outlined) style — there is no second, non-text cue (icon, color beyond fill, position at the bottom) reinforcing which button does what.

## Persona 4 — Guest who typed the wrong room number

Steps: order submitted with the wrong room number → G-06 shows the room number at 28 px, still status New → guest taps "Cancel order" → M-02 opens.

- C02 names the order number ("Your order {n} will be cancelled…"), which correctly disambiguates the order if the device holds several active ones — this works as intended and is worth noting as sound design for this persona.
- C03 reads "This cannot be undone. You can place a new order at any time," which is true, but it never mentions that "Reorder" on G-06 will pre-fill the cancelled order's items once cancellation succeeds. A guest anxious to fix a wrong room number quickly may not realize the fast path exists and may instead re-browse the whole store and re-add every product by hand, which is slower than necessary at the exact moment speed matters. **[Minor — F4]**
- If the cancellation instead returns *already accepted* (staff accepted before the wrong-room cancellation arrived), M-02 closes with no message of its own — correctly, since the "already accepted" banner belongs to G-06, not this file — but from this persona's point of view the entire reason for cancelling (wrong room) is never referenced anywhere: the guest is told only "call reception," with no acknowledgment that the room number is the specific problem. This is out of M-02's scope (owned by G-06's banner) and is reported here only for completeness, not rated against this file.
- If the cancellation returns *failed*, see the cross-cutting findings below (particularly the "no order with this number" bucketing and the overstated-certainty wording), both of which land hardest on a guest who is racing to fix a wrong delivery address.

## Persona 5 — Guest who added a product that went out of stock before submitting

This persona's problem is created and resolved entirely before submission (M-04 / G-03 / G-04), and by the time an order reaches G-06/M-02 it has already been accepted by the server with whatever stock state existed at submission time.

- M-02 has no interaction with product or stock state at all — no product list, no availability check, nothing. There is nothing for this persona to encounter on M-02 itself. **No finding raised against this file.**
- One adjacent observation, reported for completeness only since it touches M-02's boundary: if a guest later discovers (outside this file's scope, since G-06 shows no stock state) that they no longer want one item in an already-submitted order, M-02's only lever is cancelling the *entire* order — see persona 7 below.

## Persona 6 — Guest with a weak connection that drops during submission (here: during cancellation)

Steps: status New, weak signal → tap "Cancel order" → M-02 → tap "Yes, cancel" → the request is slow or drops.

- The in-flight state cannot be dismissed and has no visible countdown, elapsed-time indicator, or way to abandon the wait; the attempt is capped at 15 seconds. Because M-02's entire purpose is to beat staff acceptance to the finish line, this fixed wait consumes real time in exactly the scenario where time matters most. There is no auto-retry, so after a failure the guest must notice the new text block and manually tap "Yes, cancel" again — each additional round can cost up to another 15 seconds before the next failure is even detected. **[Medium — F5]**
- A guest who perceives the sheet as frozen (no feedback beyond a static spinner and no way to interrupt it) is a plausible candidate to reload the page out of frustration. If the cancellation had, in fact, just succeeded on the server before the reload reached the device, the "cancelled by the guest on this device" flag is never written (per the spec's own accepted-limitation note), and G-06 will subsequently display "ألغى الفندق هذا الطلب / The hotel cancelled this order" for a cancellation this guest personally performed. The order ends up in the correct (Cancelled) state — no operational error reaches staff — but the guest is told a story that is not what happened, which can read as "why did the hotel cancel my order?" and could prompt an unnecessary call to reception. Weak-connection guests are the population most likely to trigger this reload. **[Medium — F6]**
- The failure copy is a single, cause-agnostic message ("check your internet connection, then tap 'Yes, cancel' again"). For a genuinely dropped connection or timeout this is accurate and sufficient. See cross-cutting finding F-A for the one case (server says "no order with this number") where this same wording is actively misleading.

## Persona 7 — Guest who wants to change the order after submitting it

Steps: status New, guest wants to add one item / change a quantity / remove one line → the only control on G-06 that could help is "Cancel order" → M-02.

- M-02 correctly tells the guest, in plain language, that cancelling stops the whole order ("will not be brought to your room… cannot be undone") — it does not pretend to offer a partial edit, which avoids a false expectation. That said, within M-02 itself there is no distinction between "I want to change one detail" and "I want to call the whole thing off": both guests are shown the identical two sentences and the identical all-or-nothing action. A guest who only wants to add a forgotten item must cancel everything, retype the room number, re-choose payment, and re-add every previously ordered product (unless they know to use Reorder afterward — see next point) to get back to where they started plus one item. **[Medium — F7]**
- As in persona 4, C03 never mentions that "Reorder" (on G-06, after the cancellation completes) will restore the previous line items; a guest may decline to cancel purely because they believe fixing one detail means rebuilding the whole order by hand. **[Minor — F8]**
- If the order has already moved past status New by the time the guest wants to change it, "Cancel order" is not rendered on G-06 at all and M-02 is never reached — that dead end belongs to G-06/locked decision 6, not to this file, and is reported here only to confirm where this persona's real wall is.

## Cross-cutting findings (not tied to a single persona)

**F-A — "No order with this number" is folded into the generic failure message, and the message is actively wrong for that case. [Critical]**
Per §5.4, a server answer that it holds no order with this number is classified as the *failed* outcome and rendered with exactly the same copy as a dropped connection or timeout: "Check your internet connection, then tap 'Yes, cancel' again… Trying again is safe: an order cannot be cancelled twice." For this specific server answer, retrying can never succeed — there is no order to cancel, no matter how many times the request is repeated — yet the copy repeatedly points the guest at the one action guaranteed to fail again, and never suggests the action that would actually reveal the truth: closing the modal (which reads as "giving up" from the two-sentence C04 the guest is looking at), which lets G-06's own status poll surface its own, differently-worded "لم نعثر على هذا الطلب / We could not find this order" state with the correct call-reception guidance. A guest could be caught retrying indefinitely under a false "it's just my connection" diagnosis, with the true diagnosis one dismiss-tap away and nothing on screen pointing them there.

**F-B — The failure copy overstates what the device can know for two of its five failure causes. [Minor]**
Line 1 of C04 states as fact "Your order has not been cancelled and is unchanged." For a request that never left the device (no connection, dropped mid-flight, 15-second timeout) this is accurate. For an "error response" or "unreadable response," the request may well have reached the server and been processed — only the confirmation was lost — so the device cannot actually know the order is unchanged. In practice this self-heals on the next attempt (cancellation is idempotent and the retry will correctly report *cancelled* with the right on-device flag), so the consequence is limited to a moment of overstated certainty, not a lasting error.

**F-C — The failed state's only signal is new, static, colorless text above the buttons. [Minor]**
By design there is no color change, icon, highlight, or motion when C04/C05 appear (to preserve grayscale legibility and avoid startling the guest). This is a reasonable trade-off and the new text sits directly above the button the guest is about to tap again, so it is likely to be noticed — but combined with the low visual salience already noted for personas 1 and 3, it is the thinnest signal the sheet could give a sighted, non-screen-reader guest that anything went wrong.

## Consistency check against G-06 and the map

- The three outcomes G-06 expects from M-02 — *cancelled*, *already accepted*, *failed* — are exactly the three M-02's own outcome table (§5.4) produces. No fourth outcome is invented. **Consistent.**
- No second modal ever appears on either successful outcome (§6.4, §3.2); this matches map §4 decision 11 and G-06 §5.6 word for word. **Consistent.**
- M-02 does not restate G-06's "already accepted" banner copy anywhere on the sheet; the banner's two lines live only in G-06. **Consistent, no duplication found.**
- The order number, when printed on M-02, is described as "exactly as G-06 shows it" and is not independently reformatted. **Consistent.**

No conflicts with the locked decisions in `/CLAUDE.md` were found in this file.
