# Tester report — G-04 Checkout (إتمام الطلب)

Author: Tester (Agent 2)
Screen under test: `/spec/screens/G-04.md`
Method: seven personas, step-by-step walkthrough, problems only, no proposed fixes.

This screen is explicitly called out (by its own author) as the highest-stakes screen in the product. Persona 4 is treated with the most depth per instructions, and personas 5 and 6 are also examined closely since submission originates here.

---

## Persona 1 — Guest in a hurry at 2 AM who wants one product fast

**Steps:**
1. Already has one product in the cart; taps "Checkout" (B05) on G-03.
2. Arrives on G-04: empty room number, no payment selected, notes empty (first checkout in this tab).
3. Types the room number on the numeric keypad.
4. Must additionally tap "Card" or "Cash" before anything else works.
5. Taps "Submit order" (B05), fixes any inline error, taps again, M-01 opens, taps "Confirm and send."
6. Order succeeds, G-05 opens; the guest wants a second item ten minutes later, repeats the whole store → cart → checkout flow.

**Findings:**

- **[Medium]** The checkout draft is discarded on every successful submission and never carried from one order to the next (§7.7, acceptance criterion 41). A guest ordering a second or third time in the same night from the same room must retype the room number, reselect the payment method, and re-enter the cash amount every single time, even though every one of those values is identical to the order they placed minutes earlier. This is the exact persona for whom that repeated typing is the most costly.
- **[Minor]** Payment method has no default (§7.3): "Card" and "Cash" both start unselected, so a guest in a hurry always needs one extra deliberate tap before "Submit order" can succeed, even though most guests at a given hotel likely pay the same way each time. The spec gives a reasoned justification for no default (avoiding a wrong default reaching staff), so this is reported as a real, measured friction point rather than an oversight.
- No blocking issue found in the core path itself: with a valid room number typed and a payment method tapped, "Submit order" → M-01 → "Confirm and send" is the minimum number of steps the locked decisions allow (locked decision 2 mandates the confirmation modal), so no further compression is possible within the current design.

## Persona 2 — Foreign guest who cannot read Arabic

**Steps:**
1. Arrives on G-04 already in whichever language was set on G-01 (no language toggle exists here, per §5.1 and map §2.3 rule 6).
2. Reads the room number label, notes label, and the two payment option rows.
3. Wants to double-check a word before tapping "Submit order," or realizes only now (mid-checkout) that the interface is still in Arabic.
4. Looks for a way to switch language on this screen.

**Findings:**

- **[Medium]** G-04 has no language toggle, by design (§5.1: "both controls are one back-tap away on G-01"). A guest who did not switch language before reaching checkout — or who wants to re-check a specific word against the other language — has no in-place way to do so here; the only path is B01/"Edit cart" back to G-03, then further back to G-01, switch, and redo the checkout navigation. For a guest who is already uncertain about the language of the interface at the highest-stakes screen in the product, this is a real detour at exactly the wrong moment.
- **[Minor]** The English-name fallback rule carried over from G-01/G-03 ("if the English name is missing, the Arabic name is shown in the English interface too," §5.2) also applies to the C03 summary lines on G-04. A foreign guest reviewing the order summary just before paying may see one or more untranslated Arabic product names mixed into an otherwise-English screen, with no indication that this is expected rather than a glitch.
- No other issue found: payment helper lines, room number label, and every error message are confirmed present in both languages with matching meaning; digits are Western 0–9 in both languages as on G-01/G-03.

## Persona 3 — Elderly guest with poor eyesight

**Steps:**
1. Reads the order summary, room number label, notes label, and payment rows.
2. Types a room number; pauses partway through to think or to look at something else, tapping onto the notes field.
3. Types notes, watching (or not watching) the character counter.
4. Taps "Card" or "Cash," trying to see which one is now selected.

**Findings:**

- **[Medium]** F01 validates wrong-characters and length "on blur" as well as on submit (§7.1). A guest who types slowly, or who deliberately pauses mid-entry (e.g. typing "3" then looking away or tapping another field before continuing), triggers the "too short" error the moment focus leaves the field — even though they had every intention of continuing to type. For a guest whose typing itself is slower and more halting due to poor eyesight or reduced dexterity, this raises the odds of seeing an error before they were actually done, which can read as the system rejecting a value they never finished entering.
- **[Minor]** The character counter C08 is the smallest text on the screen (12 px, §7.9) and the currency label printed inside F03 is 14 px gray text (§4). Both are easy for a low-vision guest to miss — the counter matters because it is the only place the 200-character limit and the silent hard cap are communicated (§7.2), and a guest who cannot see it approaching 200 gets no other warning before typing stops working.
- **[Minor]** B03/B04 selection is shown mainly through a border-thickness change (1 px → 2 px) plus a small 24 px radio circle filling; both are fairly subtle visual differences to rely on for confirming which option is currently chosen, for a guest with reduced visual acuity, even though the rule against color-only signaling (§7.9) is otherwise respected.

## Persona 4 — Guest who typed the wrong room number

This is the single most important persona for this screen, per the task instructions. Walked through with several distinct concrete scenarios below, since "wrong room number" covers several different real situations that the spec treats identically.

### 4a. Ordinary typo / digit transposition (e.g. intends "305," types "350")

**Steps:**
1. Types "350" into F01, believing it is correct. No error appears — three digits inside the 2–5 digit bound, all valid characters (§7.1). Validation never checks the value against anything real (§5.3: "not in version 1").
2. Selects a payment method, taps "Submit order." Validation passes; M-01 opens showing "350" in large type (§7.8: "the room number shown on M-01 is byte-for-byte the value in F01").
3. Guest glances at the large number, sees a plausible-looking 3-digit number, and taps "Confirm and send" without noticing the transposition, because they already believe "350" is their room.
4. Order is sent to room 350. Staff walk to the wrong door.

**Finding:**
- **[Critical]** The only two safeguards the spec offers for this case — M-01's large-type redisplay and the cancel-while-New window on G-06 — do not actually catch a guest who is *confidently* wrong about their own room number. M-01 re-displays exactly what the guest typed; it performs no comparison against anything external, so a legibility improvement (large type) does nothing for a value the guest already believes is correct and is not proofreading against any other source. The cancellation recovery only exists if the guest realizes the mistake before staff accepts the order (locked decision 6) — a race against staff speed with no visible countdown or "how much time do I have" cue anywhere in the flow. For the highest-stakes field in the product, relying entirely on "the guest will notice their own typo while re-reading their own typo" is a thin safety net.

### 4b. Guest in a hotel with single-digit room numbers (e.g. room "7")

**Steps:**
1. Guest tries to type "7" into F01.
2. Per §7.1, minimum length is 2 digits: "Single-digit room numbers are not accepted in version 1 (this is a fixed rule, not a store setting)."
3. On blur or submit, C06 shows "رقم الغرفة قصير جدًا: رقمان على الأقل / Room number is too short: at least 2 digits" — a message that is factually true about the field's rule but has nothing to do with what is actually wrong (there is no way to type an accurate 1-digit room number at all).
4. Guest has no path forward except to invent a 2-digit value, e.g. pad it as "07," or "70," or type a nearby but different real room number.

**Finding:**
- **[Critical]** This is not a corner case of persona 4's typo problem — it is a structural incompatibility for any hotel whose room numbering includes single digits. The guest cannot express their real room number in the only field the interface offers; whatever they type instead is either an invented value with no meaning, or worse, a value that happens to coincide with a *different, real* room at the same hotel (§7.1 states leading zeros are "kept exactly as typed" and sent as a distinct text value, implying the interface itself treats "07" and "7" as potentially different rooms). No error message, no M-01 confirmation, and no cancellation window can catch this, because the system is never told anything is wrong — every value the guest could type passes validation cleanly.

### 4c. Guest in a hotel with alphanumeric room numbers (e.g. "A-12," "12B," "3B")

**Steps:**
1. Guest tries to type "A-12," "12B," or similar into F01.
2. The keyboard shown is a numeric keypad with digits only (§4, §7.1), so letters cannot even be typed on a touchscreen keyboard in the first place; a guest who pastes such a value instead sees "استخدم الأرقام فقط (0–9) / Use digits only (0–9)."
3. There is no alternate field, no "add a note if your room number is not a plain number" prompt, and no suggestion anywhere on G-04 to use the (optional, easily overlooked) notes field for a room qualifier.
4. Guest either abandons the order, or types only the numeric portion (e.g. "12" for room "12B"), producing a room number that is syntactically valid but points at a different, ambiguous, or non-existent destination.

**Finding:**
- **[Critical]** The room number field cannot represent an alphanumeric room number at all, and the spec offers no fallback path (no letter support, no prompt toward notes, no room-qualifier field). For any hotel using this numbering convention, this screen cannot produce a correct delivery address, and the guest is given no indication that this is even the underlying problem — the error message ("use digits only") reads as if the guest made a typing mistake, not as if the product does not support their hotel's room-numbering scheme.

### 4d. Guest who discovers the mistake after submitting

**Steps:**
1. Order already sent (any of scenarios 4a–4c). Guest later realizes the room number is wrong.
2. Per the map and locked decision 6, the only recovery is: cancel the order while it is still status "New" (via M-02 on G-06, not yet specced), then place a new order.
3. Guest re-adds every product from scratch (or uses "Reorder," available only once the order reaches Cancelled or Delivered status) and repeats the entire G-03 → G-04 flow.
4. On the new G-04, the checkout draft starts completely empty (§7.7: discarded on successful submission of the previous order, never carried between orders), so the guest retypes the room number, notes, and payment method again.

**Finding (cross-referenced under Persona 7, counted once there):** the repair path exists in principle but forces the guest to redo the entire checkout form even when only the room number was wrong, and it entirely depends on staff not having accepted the order yet. See Persona 7 for the full finding.

### Additional persona-4 observations

- **[Medium]** When the guest reaches G-04 via M-01's "Edit room number" (§3.1), F01 is refocused with the cursor placed at the end of the existing value, but nothing is pre-selected. A guest who wants to fully replace a wrong number must manually delete every digit first; a rushed or partial edit (e.g. deleting only some digits, or typing without deleting first) can easily leave a stray leftover digit, producing a new value that is still syntactically valid — 2 to 5 digits — but is a different, still-wrong number, with no error shown to flag the risk.
- **[Minor]** There is no helper or hint text anywhere near F01 (e.g. "check your key card or room door") to reduce the odds of a guest misremembering their own room number; the "no placeholder" rule (intentionally, to avoid a confusable example digit) leaves the field completely bare of any supporting context.
- **[Minor]** Arabic-Indic and Extended Arabic-Indic digits are silently converted to Western digits as they are typed (§7.1). A guest whose room door or key card displays Eastern Arabic numerals may see a value on M-01's large-type confirmation that looks visually different (Western digits) from what they just read on their door, which could — for exactly the persona most worried about a wrong room — introduce a moment of doubt about whether the system captured the right number, rather than reassurance.

## Persona 5 — Guest who added a product that went out of stock before submitting

**Steps:**
1. Guest's cart passed G-03's availability check earlier but one line goes out of stock in the window between arriving on G-04 and tapping "Submit order" (no re-check happens on G-04 itself, only at submission, §5.7).
2. Taps "Submit order," fields all valid, M-01 opens, taps "Confirm and send."
3. Server rejects the submission because of the stale item; M-04 "Items no longer available" opens, listing the affected product(s).
4. Guest taps "Remove them and continue"; the cart still has at least one line, so per §3.1 "the form is scrolled to the top so the rebuilt summary is visible... no toast; the shorter summary and the new total are the feedback."
5. Guest is now looking at G-04 again, form fields intact, order **not yet sent**.

**Findings:**

- **[Critical]** Nothing on G-04 (or, per the spec provided, on M-04) tells the guest that their order was **not sent** and that they must tap "Submit order" again. The guest has just been through a modal interaction that feels, from a distance, like part of a confirmation flow (they saw a message about their products, made a choice, and landed back on the checkout screen). "Submit order" (B05) looks exactly as it always does — same label, same styling, no urgency cue, no highlight — so a guest who assumes the interaction they just completed *was* the submission has a real chance of closing the tab or navigating away believing an order is on its way, when in fact nothing was ever sent to staff. This is a silent journey break: the guest thinks they ordered, staff never receive anything, and there is no error state anywhere to catch it.
- **[Medium]** Once back on G-04, there is no persistent record of which item(s) were just removed. The guest saw the list once, inside M-04, which is now closed; G-04 itself offers no "the following item(s) were removed" line, banner, or highlighted diff in the summary — only the shorter list and the new, lower total (§6.3 item 2, §5.2). A guest who did not fully register the M-04 list (plausible, since it appeared right after a "Confirm and send" tap, a moment of low attention) has no easy way to check afterward what changed without comparing the new summary against memory.
- **[Minor]** If a product's price also changed alongside its stock status, no equivalent notice exists (a caveat inherited from G-03's own "no price-changed notice" decision); the rebuilt summary on G-04 would simply show a different line total with no flag that anything besides the item list changed.

## Persona 6 — Guest with a weak connection that drops during submission

**Steps:**
1. Fills the form correctly on G-04, taps "Submit order," M-01 opens, taps "Confirm and send."
2. The connection drops while the request is in flight; the client never receives a response. M-03 "Order not sent" opens.
3. Guest is uncertain whether the order actually reached the server before the connection dropped.
4. Guest either taps "Retry" (same client order key, per §7.7, so a true retry cannot create a duplicate), or taps "Close" and is returned to G-04 with every value kept (§3.1), and, out of habit or frustration, tweaks a field (e.g. re-types the room number "just to be safe," or changes the payment method) before tapping "Submit order" again.
5. Alternatively: frustrated by the dropped connection, the guest closes the browser tab intending to try again once the signal improves, or the mobile OS reclaims the backgrounded tab on its own.

**Findings:**

- **[Critical]** The client order key is explicitly "replaced by a new key whenever any value that is part of the order changes" after M-01 first opened (§7.7). This is exactly the situation a weak connection creates: the guest cannot tell whether the failed request actually succeeded server-side before the response was lost. If it did succeed, and the guest then edits any field before resubmitting (a very plausible reaction — "let me just double-check the room number" is a natural response to a failure), the next submission carries a brand-new client order key, which the duplicate-safe mechanism (§7.7, map §4 decision 10) is specifically designed to treat as *not* a duplicate. The result is two real orders for what the guest believes was one attempt, with nothing on G-04, M-03, or anywhere else in the spec suggesting the guest check for an existing order (e.g. via the G-01 active-order banner) before editing and resubmitting. For a screen whose stated purpose is to prevent operational errors reaching staff, an unmarked duplicate delivery is exactly that kind of error, and it is the direct product of combining the edit-clears-idempotency rule with a connection failure mode the rule was not designed around.
- **[Medium]** The checkout draft is stored "in storage that lives only as long as this browser tab" and is "discarded... when the browser tab is closed" (§7.7). A guest on a weak connection is disproportionately likely to close the tab (a natural response to something that "seems stuck") or to have the tab reclaimed by the mobile OS while backgrounded (e.g. while checking signal strength or switching apps to test connectivity) — and in either case loses the entire draft: room number, notes, payment choice, and any cash amount. This guest, already dealing with a bad connection, is the one most likely to have to retype the whole form from zero.
- **[Minor]** The spec does not state whether "the browser tab is closed" is meant to also cover a mobile browser silently discarding/suspending a backgrounded tab (common during exactly the kind of connectivity troubleshooting this persona would do), leaving it unclear whether the draft would actually survive that very plausible scenario.

## Persona 7 — Guest who wants to change the order after submitting it

**Steps:**
1. Order already submitted and sent. Guest wants to change something (e.g. add an item, fix a mistaken payment method, or fix a room number, overlapping with Persona 4d).
2. Per the map, no in-place editing exists; the only path is cancel while status is New (M-02 on G-06), then place a new order, or use "Reorder" from G-06 once the order reaches Delivered or Cancelled.
3. Guest reaches G-04 again for the corrected order.

**Findings:**

- **[Medium]** The repair path restores the previous order's *cart lines* via Reorder (map §4 decision 7), but not the checkout-level values — room number, notes, payment method, and cash amount are never carried from one checkout to the next (§7.7: "never carried from one order to the next"). A guest who cancelled specifically because of a single wrong field (say, the payment method) must still retype every other field on G-04 from scratch, including the ones that were already correct on the first attempt.
- No G-04-specific confusion was found beyond this: the screen does not offer, hint at, or accidentally suggest any "edit my existing order" capability, so it stays consistent with the locked "no editing after submission" decision. The friction above is the direct, structural cost of that decision as expressed specifically on this screen.

## Cross-cutting checks (not tied to a single persona)

**Notes field silent truncation (§7.2):**
- **[Medium]** Pasting text longer than the remaining room in F02 is "cut to fit" with no message of any kind — "no error message for notes" is stated explicitly (§7.2, §5.4). A guest who pastes a longer note (e.g. copied delivery instructions from another app, or a longer explanation added at the end of existing text) may have meaningful content silently cut off, and the only way to notice is to look at the 12 px counter (C08) after pasting — easy to miss, especially combined with the low-vision concern already raised under Persona 3. Because there is no error message by design, there is no mechanism at all to flag this loss to the guest.

**"Amount you will pay with" below total blocks rather than warns (§7.4):**
- **[Minor]** The design choice to block (rather than warn) is reasoned in the spec itself and is defensible for the field's stated purpose ("so staff can bring change"). However, the field and its error message never state that split payment (e.g. paying part by card, part by cash) is not supported; a guest who intends exactly that reads the blocking message ("Enter the full amount you will pay, or leave this field empty") as if they made an entry mistake, rather than realizing the product simply has no path for a mixed payment.

**No unit price and no expander in the order summary (§5.2, §5.7):**
- **[Minor]** With no unit price shown anywhere in the summary (only "{qty} × {name}" and a line total), a guest who wants to spot-check whether a line total looks right has no way to verify it against a per-unit price without leaving G-04 (back to G-03) — a deliberate simplification per the spec's own decision list, but a real loss of at-a-glance verifiability on the one screen where the guest is about to commit to paying that total.

**Payment default absence, room-number bound checks, digit rules, tap targets, and text sizes:**
- No further issues found beyond those already raised above under Persona 1 (no default payment) and Persona 4 (2–5 digit bound). Field text sizes (F01 24 px, F02/F03 16 px) meet the ≥16 px anti-auto-zoom rule for every actual input; every inline error uses the "!" icon plus a 2 px border rather than color alone (§7.9), consistent with the same rule on G-01/G-03; every tappable element's stated tap area meets 44 × 44 px; currency formatting ("62.50 ر.س" / "SAR 62.50") and Western digits match G-01's and G-03's conventions exactly. No contradiction was found between G-04 and the map's stated route in (G-03 → "Checkout") or out (M-01 → G-05 / M-03 / M-04).

### Critical findings
1. **(Persona 4b)** Single-digit hotel room numbers (e.g. room "7") cannot be entered at all — the 2-digit minimum forces the guest to submit an invented or a different real room's number, with no error ever raised because the padded value is syntactically valid.
2. **(Persona 4c)** Alphanumeric room numbers (e.g. "A-12," "12B") cannot be represented in F01 at all — the numeric-only keypad and digit-only validation give no fallback path, so this screen cannot produce a correct delivery address for such hotels.
3. **(Persona 4a)** M-01's large-type confirmation and the cancel-while-New recovery do not protect against a guest who is *confidently* wrong about their own room number (e.g. a transposition they don't notice); the confirmation only re-displays what was typed and performs no external check, and the cancellation window depends entirely on staff not having accepted the order yet, with no visible time budget shown to the guest.
4. **(Persona 5)** After M-04 "Remove them and continue," nothing tells the guest their order was not sent and must be resubmitted; "Submit order" gives no urgency cue, creating a real risk that the guest believes the order already went through and abandons the screen, so no order ever reaches staff.
5. **(Persona 6)** The client order key is replaced whenever any field changes after M-01 opened; combined with a dropped connection where the failed request may have actually succeeded server-side, a guest who edits anything before retrying can unknowingly create a second, genuine duplicate order with nothing in the flow prompting them to check for an existing order first.
