# Tester report — G-07 My orders (طلباتي)

Author: Tester (Agent 2)
Screen under test: `/spec/screens/G-07.md`
Referenced: `/spec/screens/G-01.md` (canonical status labels, active-order banner), `/spec/screens/M-01.md` (shape of the saved order record)
Method: seven personas, step-by-step walkthrough, problems only, no proposed fixes.

---

## Persona 1 — Guest in a hurry at 2 AM who wants one product fast

**Steps:**
1. This persona's primary goal (add one product, checkout) does not pass through G-07 at all; G-07 is reached only by deliberately tapping "طلباتي / My orders" on G-01.
2. Assume the guest already submitted an order earlier and now, still in a hurry, wants a fast confirmation that it went through. Taps G-01-B02.
3. G-07 renders on the first frame with no skeleton or spinner (§6.1) — the list is read from the device synchronously, which is genuinely fast for this persona.
4. Sees the order row with its status pill, taps it to open G-06 for the live detail.

**Findings:**
- **[Minor]** G-07 is a full screen with its own history entry (§1), not a lightweight sheet or a section of G-01. A hurried guest who only wanted a one-glance confirmation must make two extra taps (open G-07, then back arrow) to return to shopping, instead of a dismissible overlay. This is an architecture choice already fixed by the map, but it is friction for exactly this persona.
- No blocking issue found: the no-skeleton rule (§6.1) is a genuine speed advantage for this persona compared to G-01's up-to-10-second catalog wait.

## Persona 2 — Foreign guest who cannot read Arabic

**Steps:**
1. Normal path: guest already set the toggle to English on G-01; the choice persists (§1, "the language currently saved on the device"), so G-07 renders in English with no toggle needed here.
2. Edge path per §3.1: "Browser reload on G-07, or the G-07 URL opened directly." If this is the very first interaction with the store on this device (no saved language choice — e.g., a shared/bookmarked link that opens straight on the G-07 URL, or a browser that reopens its last tab), G-07 renders in Arabic by default (locked decision 7) with no on-screen way to change it, because "G-07 has no language toggle; the toggle lives on G-01 and G-08 only" (§1, §5.1).
3. The guest must recognize the icon-only back arrow (B01, no text, so at least it does not require reading) and tap it to reach G-01, where the toggle finally exists.

**Findings:**
- **[Medium]** For the direct-URL/reload-with-no-saved-language case the spec itself allows for (§3.1 row 3), a foreign guest lands on an all-Arabic screen — title, row labels, status pills, empty-state copy — with zero on-screen means to switch language. The icon-only back arrow is their only way out, and it is not obviously "the language settings are on the next screen" to someone who has never used the product before. This is a real, if edge-case, dead end in that language remains wrong until they navigate away.
- No other issue found on the mainstream path (guest arrives from G-01 with a language already chosen): status labels, currency, dates and item-count wording are all confirmed bilingual and consistent with G-01 §5.2/§7.2/§7.3.

## Persona 3 — Elderly guest with poor eyesight

**Steps:**
1. Opens G-07, sees the list of order rows: order number (16 px bold), status pill (14 px), date/time (14 px), total (14 px bold), item count and room (14 px).
2. Tries to distinguish, at a glance, which orders are still active and which are finished, per the row design's own stated goal (§5.3: "a guest scanning the list sees at once which rows are still moving without reading every label").
3. Specifically tries to tell apart a "Delivered" row from a "Cancelled" row without reading the pill text closely.

**Findings:**
- **[Medium]** Per §5.3, Delivered is an outlined pill with **dark** text and Cancelled is an outlined pill with **gray** text — both are the same shape and fill (outlined, unfilled), differing only by text-color weight. For a guest with poor eyesight, this is exactly the kind of subtle distinction that is hard to catch at a glance, even though it technically satisfies the 4.5:1 contrast rule and is "not color alone" by the letter of the rule. The screen's own design goal ("sees at once which rows are still moving") explicitly does not extend to telling Delivered apart from Cancelled — for those two specific states the guest must actually read the word, which is the harder of the two tasks for this persona.
- No other issue found: minimum text size of 14 px, row height of at least 100 px, and full-width tap targets are all more generous than G-01's smaller controls, which is a genuine improvement for this persona compared to G-01's 40 px steppers.

## Persona 4 — Guest who typed the wrong room number

**Steps:**
1. Guest recalls they may have mistyped the room number at checkout and opens G-07 to check.
2. Finds the order row; C07 shows the room number exactly as typed, leading zeros included (§5.2 item 6, §7.1) — e.g. "منتجان · الغرفة 0305".
3. Confirms the number is indeed wrong.
4. Looks on the row itself for any way to correct it or flag it.

**Findings:**
- **[Minor]** G-07 correctly and honestly surfaces the exact room number the order was sent to, which does let this persona confirm their mistake without opening the order (this part works as intended). However, the row offers no next step at all — no "this looks wrong," no shortcut to reception, no inline hint. The guest must tap into G-06 to discover whatever options exist there (cancel while New, per locked decision 6). This is consistent with the map's decision to keep G-07 to reading only, but it means a guest who has just confirmed their exact worry on this screen gets no acknowledgment of it here, only a silent number.

## Persona 5 — Guest who added a product that went out of stock before submitting

Not applicable to this screen. A record only exists on G-07 after a **successful** submission (M-01 §7.4 step 1); an item that went out of stock before submitting is caught by G-03's stock re-check or by M-04 at submission time, both of which occur before any record is written. G-07 therefore never shows an incomplete, rejected, or partially-submitted order, and there is nothing for this persona to encounter on this screen. No finding.

## Persona 6 — Guest with a weak connection that drops during submission

The actual drop during submission is handled on G-04/M-01/M-03, not on G-07. Reporting only what G-07 itself does for a guest on a weak connection, and what they see if they land here:

**Steps:**
1. Guest has an order already in flight (e.g. status "New") and a currently weak/unreliable connection.
2. Opens G-07. The list renders instantly regardless of connection quality, since it is read from the device (§6.1) — this is a real strength for this persona: they are never blocked from seeing their order history by a bad connection.
3. The status refresh (§5.5) is sent once, with a 10-second limit; on a weak connection it is likely to fail or time out.
4. Per §5.5, on failure "the rows keep their last known status and no error text appears anywhere."

**Findings:**
- **[Medium]** The row's status pill never indicates whether what is shown is the live status or a last-known value that failed to refresh (§5.5 explicitly: "the guest cannot tell which, and the label 'last known' is never rendered"). On a weak connection, where the refresh is more likely to fail silently, a guest could act on (or simply believe) a status that is meaningfully out of date — for example still seeing "جديد / New" and assuming cancellation is still possible, when the real server-side status has already moved past it. This is a real risk specifically for this persona.
- **[Minor]** When the refresh does succeed, the pill updates in place with "no sound, no highlight, no toast" (§5.5). A guest who is looking directly at the row when this happens sees the status text change with no visual cue that anything occurred, which could read as if they misread the label a moment earlier rather than as a live update.
- No finding that a weak connection breaks G-07's core rendering or navigation: the screen is drawn from local storage and never depends on a live request to be usable (§6.1, §3.1).

## Persona 7 — Guest who wants to change the order after submitting it

No editing of a submitted order exists anywhere in the spec by design (map decision, cancel-while-New via M-02 on G-06 is the only lever). Reporting what G-07 itself contributes to or fails to clarify for this persona:

**Steps:**
1. Guest wants to know whether it is still possible to change or stop their order. Opens G-07.
2. Looks at the status pill to judge whether the order might still be cancellable (locked decision 6: only while status is New).
3. Sees a dark-filled pill reading "جديد / New," "تم القبول وجارٍ التحضير / Accepted & preparing," or "في الطريق / On the way" — all three share the identical pill styling (§5.3: "the three active statuses share one look").

**Findings:**
- **[Medium]** The row visually groups all three active statuses identically (same fill, same weight), and the only way to know whether cancellation is still possible is to read and correctly interpret the text label itself, then know the business rule that only "New" is cancellable. A guest scanning quickly (which is exactly what the pill's design is optimized for, per §5.3) gets no visual signal that "New" is meaningfully different in capability from "Accepted & preparing" or "On the way" — they must tap into G-06 to actually discover whether the "Cancel order" control exists for that order.
- No new finding beyond what is already established for the product as a whole (G-01's test report already flags, as Critical, that there is no way anywhere in the spec to add to or edit an existing order — only cancel-while-New or reorder after completion). G-07 is consistent with that design; it adds no separate control and does not misrepresent what is possible, it simply requires an extra tap to G-06 to find out.

## Cross-cutting checks (not tied to a single persona)

**Reorder is not on rows (map §4 decision 8):**
- Confirmed absent. G-07 has no reorder control anywhere (§3.2, §4, §5.8); it exists only on G-06 for Delivered/Cancelled orders. No finding — this matches the instruction and is implemented as stated.

**List cap and deletion (§5.4):**
- **[Medium]** Trim rule 2 states: "If more than 20 records remain and none of them is Delivered or Cancelled: delete the oldest record regardless of status." The spec itself calls this "not a real situation," but if it ever occurred, an **active, trackable order** could be silently deleted from the device with zero notification to the guest — no warning before, no message after, and C08's cap note only ever reads "keeps only your last 20 orders," which does not distinguish "we dropped an old finished order" from "we dropped a still-active one you might have wanted to cancel or track." This is an edge case, but the consequence (permanently losing the only interface path to cancel or track an in-progress order) is serious if it is ever hit.
- **[Medium]** Once a record is trimmed (even in the ordinary Delivered/Cancelled case), its order number is gone from the device with no recall mechanism. C08 only appears when exactly 20 rows are currently shown, so it functions as a static heads-up at the moment of full capacity, not a per-deletion notice; a guest who later wants to reference a specific old order (e.g. a billing question) and cannot recall its exact number has no on-device way to look it up once it has been trimmed.

**Empty state and the honest limitation of locked decision 4 (§5.7, §6.2):**
- **[Medium]** The empty-state copy is identical regardless of *why* the list is empty: never ordered from this device, cleared site data, a different device, or an ended private session (§5.7's own table lists all four as landing on the same three lines). For a guest who specifically lost access mid-flight — e.g. site data was cleared while an order was still status "New" — the copy says only "Only orders sent from this device appear here" and points to the room phone; it never specifically surfaces that the practical effect is the guest has now lost their only interface-based way to cancel that order. The instruction to call reception does cover the underlying need, but the guest is not told *why* they suddenly cannot see or manage an order they know exists.

**Five canonical status labels, digits, tap targets, toggle:**
- Status labels (§5.3, §7.1) are reused verbatim from G-01 §5.2 in both languages — confirmed consistent, no finding.
- All numbers (order number, date, time, total, item count, room number) use Western digits per §7.1/§7.2/§9 — confirmed consistent, no finding.
- Tap targets: rows ≥100 px tall and full width, B01 44×44 px, B02 240×48 px — all meet or exceed the 44×44 px convention, no finding.
- No language toggle on G-07 by design (map §2.3 rule 6) — consistent with the rule; the only issue found is the direct-entry edge case already reported under Persona 2.

### Medium findings (no Critical found on this screen)
1. No language toggle exists on G-07, and a direct/reload entry with no saved language choice can strand a foreign guest in Arabic with only an icon-only back arrow as an escape (Persona 2).
2. Delivered and Cancelled rows are both outlined pills differing only by text-color weight, undermining the row design's own "see at once which rows are still moving" goal for exactly the two statuses an elderly guest most wants to distinguish (Persona 3).
3. The status pill never indicates live vs. last-known, so a guest on a weak connection (where silent refresh failures are more likely) can act on a stale status without any cue that it might be stale (Persona 6).
4. All three active statuses (New, Accepted & preparing, On the way) share identical pill styling, giving no visual signal about which orders are still cancellable under locked decision 6 (Persona 7).
5. The cap-trim rule can, in an edge case the spec itself calls unrealistic, silently delete a still-active order with no warning before or after.
6. A trimmed record's order number becomes permanently unrecoverable on-device, with no per-deletion notice, only a static "keeps last 20" note.
7. The empty state gives identical generic copy whether the guest never ordered or lost access to an in-progress order, without surfacing that cancellation access specifically may now be gone.
