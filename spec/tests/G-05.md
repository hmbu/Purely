# Tester findings — G-05 Order submitted

Author: Tester (Agent 2)
Screen file tested: `/spec/screens/G-05.md` (status: Draft)
Cross-referenced: `/spec/screens/G-01.md` (Approved — canonical status labels, conventions), `/spec/screens/G-04.md`, `/spec/screens/M-01.md` (both Draft, referenced for the room-number safety chain and the client-order-key contract), `/spec/screens-map.md`.
Method: walked the screen as each of the seven personas in `CLAUDE.md` §"Agent 2 — Tester". Persona 4 (wrong room number) and persona 7 (wants to change the order) were given the deepest walk-through, per instruction, since G-05 is the second of the two screens where a guest first meets the delivered order after submission. Findings are reported only; no fixes are proposed.

---

## Persona 1 — Guest in a hurry at 2 AM, wants one product fast

Steps: taps "Submit order" → M-01 → "Confirm and send" → lands on G-05.

- The screen tells them plainly the order was sent (checkmark, title), and offers exactly two exits ("Track order" / "Back to store"), which is fast to parse.
- **Finding G-05-01 (Minor).** No estimated delivery time or ETA of any kind is shown (deliberately, per §5.5 of this file). A guest who wants "one product fast" has no way to judge, from this screen, how long the wait will be. The file already records this as a possible `/spec/backlog.md` candidate; noted here for completeness, not as a new gap.
- The screen requires reading the room number and one extra sentence (C05) before either button is reachable in the visual order — see finding G-05-02 below, which is really a persona-4 concern but affects this hurried persona's likelihood of registering it.
- No other friction found for this persona: the screen sends no request, so there is nothing to wait for.

## Persona 2 — Foreign guest who cannot read Arabic

Steps: same entry. G-05 has no language toggle of its own (confirmed, §5.1: "map §2.3 rule 6").

- If the guest already switched to English on G-01 before checking out, every string on G-05 is in English and the numbers are language-independent (Western digits both ways). No problem found in that path.
- **Finding G-05-02 (Medium).** If the guest reaches checkout and submission while the interface is still in the Arabic default (locked decision 7) — for instance because they didn't notice the small header toggle on G-01, or because the toggle only shows the *other* language's name and they didn't parse that convention — G-05 gives them **no way to switch language** and no visible cue that a toggle exists at all (there's no "language" icon, no hint text). The only recovery is to use the back arrow / "Back to store" to reach G-01, find the toggle there, and then navigate back into tracking via the active-order banner or "My orders". This is a documented, deliberate cross-screen decision (map §2.3 rule 6), not a bug on this file alone, but it means a confused foreign guest's confirmation screen, order number, and payment instructions are all read in a language they may not understand, with no in-place remedy.

## Persona 3 — Elderly guest with poor eyesight

Steps: same entry.

- Text sizing is generous by convention (minimum 14 px on this screen, room number 48 px bold, high-contrast rules stated). Pinch-zoom and text scaling are explicitly not disabled. No problem found with the base typography rules.
- **Finding G-05-03 (Medium).** The file's own "Fit rule" (§7.5) computes the sum of every element's height at a 390 × 664 px viewport and arrives at 658 px — only **6 px of slack** below the stated 664 px viewport, and this sum does **not** include the device safe-area inset that the very same file calls out elsewhere (§4, G-05-S03 row: "16 px bottom padding **plus the device's safe-area inset**"). On a notched phone the safe-area inset alone (commonly ~34 px) would exceed the remaining 6 px of slack, meaning acceptance criterion 19 ("the entire content including B03 is visible without scrolling") is unlikely to hold on a large share of real devices even at default text size — before considering that an elderly guest is more likely than average to have enlarged system text, which the file itself acknowledges will force scrolling. The claim is precise-looking but the arithmetic omits a value the file states exists.
- A guest with enlarged text will need to scroll to reach "Back to store" (acknowledged in the file); this is accepted behavior per the file, not a new finding, but it compounds the point above.

## Persona 4 — Guest who typed the wrong room number

This is one of the two decisive personas for this screen; walked in depth.

Steps: on G-04 the guest mistypes "350" for "305". On M-01 they see "350" at 64 px and, being rushed or simply not scrutinizing it, tap "Confirm and send" without catching the error (this is exactly the scenario the room-number chain is designed to still catch downstream). The order is sent. G-05 opens.

- G-05 shows the same room number, "350", now at 48 px, directly under the title, with C05 immediately below it: "رقم الغرفة غير صحيح؟ يمكنك إلغاء الطلب من صفحة المتابعة ما دام الفندق لم يقبله بعد / Wrong room number? You can cancel the order from the tracking page until the hotel accepts it." This is a real, working second chance, and the wording correctly names the exact remedy (cancel via tracking) and the exact deadline (before acceptance).
- **Finding G-05-04 (Medium).** The safety net depends entirely on the guest reading two numbers in a row that look the same and a sentence about what to do if they're wrong. Nothing on G-05 requires or nudges any interaction with the room-number area itself (it is explicitly "not editable, not a control," §4) beyond the same passive reading that already failed to catch the mistake once, seconds earlier, on M-01. A guest who tapped through M-01 without reading is not obviously more likely to read the *second*, near-identical presentation of the same number attentively. The mechanism is sound in principle but its real-world catch rate depends on an assumption (repetition increases the odds of reading) that is not obviously true for a distracted or hurried guest.
- Walking the actual repair: if the guest does notice the wrong number, they must tap "Track order" (not "Back to store") to reach the only place the cancel control exists (G-06). If instead they tap "Back to store" — the visually secondary but still full-width, equally reachable button directly below "Track order" — they land on G-01, where the correction path is one step further away (they must notice the active-order banner and tap it to reach G-06). Both paths eventually work, but "Back to store" is a plausible, likely tap for a guest who has just been told "your order was sent" and reflexively wants to close the loop and keep browsing — and it is the button that removes the guest one extra step from the fix.
- No blocking or dead end was found: whichever button is tapped, the order remains cancellable from G-06 as long as status is still New, and both G-05's C05 and (per the cross-file contract this file states as binding) G-06 continue to make the room number visible next to the cancel control. The finding above is about attentiveness and path length, not about a missing control.

## Persona 5 — Guest who added a product that went out of stock before submitting

Steps: attempted checkout with an item that went out of stock.

- This scenario is fully resolved upstream, before G-05 can ever be reached: out-of-stock rejection at submission time is handled by M-04, and G-05 is only shown after a **success** response (§3.1: "Success response to Confirm and send" / "Retry"). By the time this screen exists for a given order, every line in it was accepted by the server.
- **No finding.** G-05 itself does nothing right or wrong here — the persona's trouble genuinely lives entirely in M-04 and G-04, not in this file. Stated plainly per instruction: this screen has no exposure to this scenario at all.

## Persona 6 — Guest with a weak connection that drops during submission

Steps: connection drops while "Confirm and send" is in flight.

- G-05 sends **no request on any entry** (§3.1, §5.0: "G-05 fetches nothing from the server"), and it is only ever shown after M-01 or M-03 has already received a success response and written the order record to the device. A dropped connection during submission is entirely M-01/M-03's domain (timeout, retry, duplicate-safe key) and never reaches this screen in a half-finished state.
- The file also states a reload of G-05 while offline still renders correctly from the device record (§3.3), which was checked and holds up: nothing on this screen depends on connectivity once it is shown.
- **No finding.** As with persona 5, this screen has no exposure to this scenario; stated plainly rather than inventing a fault.

## Persona 7 — Guest who wants to change the order after submitting it

This is the second decisive persona for this screen.

Steps: guest realizes, on G-05, that they forgot to add a product, chose the wrong payment method, or wrote a wrong note — not a room-number problem.

- **Finding G-05-05 (Medium).** C05, the only "something is wrong, here's the fix" messaging on this screen, is worded and scoped exclusively around the room number ("Wrong room number? You can cancel the order from the tracking page..."). Locked decision 6 in fact allows the guest to cancel a New order for **any** reason, not only a wrong room number, and G-06's Cancel control (per this file's own cross-reference, §5.3) is unconditional on the reason. But a guest reading G-05 who wants to fix a forgotten item, a wrong payment method, or wrong notes is given no equivalent sentence telling them cancellation (then reorder) is also their remedy for those cases. The escape hatch that exists is real, but its visible framing on this screen is narrower than its actual scope, which risks a guest who has a non-room-number complaint concluding there is no way to fix it.
- Walking the actual (if unadvertised) repair for this persona: "Track order" → G-06 → "Cancel order" (while status is New) → M-02 confirms → G-06 shows Cancelled → "Reorder" appears → items go to G-03, where they can be freely edited (quantities, removals, additions) → checkout again with a clean, unprefilled room number, notes, and payment selection (G-04's "no pre-fill" rule, confirmed against G-04 §5.3). This chain does work end-to-end for correcting items, payment method, and notes, provided the mistake is caught before staff accept. G-05 itself never states any of this beyond the room-number-specific sentence.
- No finding beyond the one above: G-05 correctly sends nothing and holds no state that would need to be "edited" — its role is a one-time confirmation, and it fulfills that role; the gap is purely in what it tells the guest about their options.
