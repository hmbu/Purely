**Why this screen exists at all.** The guest who sees it has done nothing wrong and has nothing to browse: there is no catalog, so there is no header with a hotel name, no category bar, no product list, no cart bar. Everything G-01 is made of is missing. G-08 is what is left: one sentence saying what happened, one sentence saying what to do, — in the case where trying again can work — one button, and, for the guest who already has an order on this device, one way out to the list of those orders (B03), which needs no catalog to render. It carries the language toggle because a guest who cannot load the store must still be able to read **this** screen in their own language; without the toggle here, a guest whose device has never saved a choice would meet an Arabic-only dead end on their first scan (locked decision 7 makes Arabic the first render).

## 1. ID and name

- **ID:** G-08
- **Name:** المتجر غير متاح / Store unavailable
- **Type:** Full guest screen, and the **only** screen that replaces another screen in place. It has **no URL of its own** (§3.3): it takes over the viewport at whatever store URL the guest is on.
- **Two variants, one screen** (map §4 decision 13): the **retryable** variant and the **invalid-link** variant. They share the header, the layout, the icon, the reception line, the language toggle and the "طلباتي / My orders" control B03 (identical element, identical condition, identical placement on both); they differ in the title, the body line, when the reception line appears, and the presence of the "إعادة المحاولة / Retry" button (§5.2).
- **Language:** Arabic (RTL) on first open regardless of browser language; English (LTR) if English was saved on the device. The toggle B01 switches it in place and saves the choice, exactly as on G-01 (G-01 §7.4). G-08 and G-01 are the only two screens with a toggle (map §4 decision 12).

---

## 2. Purpose

Tell the guest, in their language, that the store cannot open and give them the one action that can help: try again, or ask reception.

## 3. Entry and exit points

### 3.1 Entry (how the guest arrives)

| From | Trigger | Variant shown |
|---|---|---|
| The QR scan (first open) | The catalog request fails, is unreadable, or does not answer within 10 seconds (G-01 §6.1) | **Retryable** |
| The QR scan, or any opened link | The URL is not a valid store link (§7.1 classifier) | **Invalid link** |
| G-01 Store | A browser reload whose catalog fetch fails (G-01 §3.2 last row) | **Retryable** |
| G-02 Product details | A browser reload or a direct product URL whose catalog fetch fails (G-02 §3.1; map §2.2 G-02 row) | **Retryable** |
| G-03 Cart | A browser reload or a direct cart URL whose catalog fetch fails (G-03 §3.1, §6.1 case (a)) | **Retryable** |
| G-08 itself | "إعادة المحاولة / Retry" failed again (§5.4) | **Retryable**, re-rendered with C04 now shown |

G-06 and G-07 never lead here: neither fetches the catalog (G-07 §3.1, G-06 §3.1). G-04 and the four modals never lead here either: they are reached only from a screen whose catalog already loaded. That sentence forbids the forward route only — **the reverse route does exist: B03 leads from here to G-07, which needs no catalog** (§3.2, §5.1). The one way a guest who left through B03 can see G-08 again is the browser back from that G-07, which re-loads the URL whose load failed (§3.3).

**What G-08 does not disturb.** Arriving here changes nothing the device holds: the cart, the saved orders, the language choice and the session catalog copy are all left exactly as they were (locked decision 4; map §2.3 rules 4–6). A guest whose reload failed still has their cart when the store opens again.

### 3.2 Exit (where each action leads)

| Action on G-08 | Destination |
|---|---|
| Tap "إعادة المحاولة / Retry" (B02), retryable variant only | Stays on G-08 in its trying state (§6.1) while one catalog request runs. **Success →** G-01 Store, scrolled to the top, replacing G-08 (§3.3). **Failure →** the retryable variant again, with B02 active and C04 now shown (§5.4) |
| Tap "طلباتي / My orders" (B03), on **either** variant, whenever it is rendered | G-07 My orders, at the top of the list — always the list, never a single order and never G-06, even when the device holds exactly one record. | Tap the language toggle (B01) | Stays on G-08; the whole screen re-renders in the other language and direction, the choice is saved on the device, and a Retry already in flight keeps running |

G-08 leads to exactly two screens inside the store: **G-01**, through a successful Retry or a successful reload, and **G-07**, through B03. It leads to no other screen and opens no modal.

### 3.3 URL, history, and reload

- **No URL of its own.** G-08 renders in place of the screen that failed and leaves the address bar untouched. A modal has no URL because it sits on top of a screen (map §2.3 rule 2); G-08 has none because it *is* a screen that stands in for another one, and giving it a URL would let a guest bookmark or share "the hotel store is broken".
- **On a successful Retry**, G-01 replaces the G-08 entry in the history: the address bar becomes the store root, and a browser back from that G-01 goes to whatever was beneath (usually nothing — the guest leaves the store, as G-01 §3.1 states). G-08 is never returned to by back.
- **A browser reload while G-08 is shown** re-runs the load of the URL in the address bar. That is not the same action as B02: a reload of a product URL that succeeds lands on **G-02**, while B02 always lands on **G-01**. Both are correct; B02 is the button the spec offers, the reload is the browser's own affordance, and neither can produce a state where the guest is stuck.
- **B03 is a normal navigation to G-07's own URL** and pushes a history entry above the URL whose load failed; G-08 still has no URL and never writes the address bar. Back from that G-07 loads the failed URL again: on success the guest lands on G-01, G-02 or G-03 as that URL dictates; on failure G-08 is shown again as a **fresh visit**, with C04 hidden on the retryable variant. G-07 §3.1 and §3.2 state the same rule from the other side.
- **Why B02 lands on G-01 and not on the screen that failed** (map §2.2, G-08 row): the guest on this screen has no catalog, no cart bar and no navigation. G-01 is the only screen that can be entered from nothing, and it holds the way to everything else — categories, the cart, "My orders", the active-order banner. Returning them to a deep product URL after a failure would be a narrower answer to a wider problem.
- **Offline:** everything on G-08 renders from the interface itself; there is nothing to fetch in order to draw it, so the screen is always complete, in both variants, with or without a connection.

## 5. Content

### 5.1 Header (S01) — what it holds and what it does not

The header holds **exactly two controls and nothing else**: B01 at the start edge (right in Arabic, left in English) and B03 at the end edge (left in Arabic, right in English), each vertically centred and 16 px from its edge, on **both variants**. When B03 is not rendered the header holds B01 alone.

- **B01, the language toggle**, at the start edge. Reason (map §1.3, §4 decision 12): a guest whose first contact with the product is this screen has never had a chance to choose a language, and locked decision 7 renders Arabic first. Without a toggle here, a guest who reads only English would be told, in a script they cannot read, that something is wrong and what to do about it. G-01 and G-08 are therefore the two screens that carry it, and for the same reason: they are the two screens a guest can arrive at from outside the product.
- **B03, "طلباتي / My orders"**, at the end edge, on both variants. Reason (map §2.2 G-08 row as amended): G-07 and G-06 render entirely from the device and need no catalog, so a catalog failure must never consume the cancel-before-acceptance window locked decision 6 grants the guest. The order was reachable the whole time; only the route was missing. The label is character-for-character the label of G-01-B02 — `طلباتي` / `My orders` — and is never a new string; only the treatment differs (a 16 px text button here, a 24 px icon over a 10 px label on G-01), because G-08 has no icon set of its own and its text floor is 14 px.
- **B03 is rendered if and only if the device order list holds at least one readable record** (the readability test of G-07 §6.3 case 2). The test is evaluated on arrival and again on every re-render — a language switch, a failed Retry — and it is synchronous: it reads the device and sends nothing. When the device holds zero readable records, or the storage cannot be read at all, B03 is not rendered on either variant: not greyed, not disabled, not present. **The condition is the existence of a record, never its status**: a device holding only Delivered or Cancelled orders shows B03 too, and G-08 never reads a status.
- **No hotel name.** The hotel name comes from the store settings that arrive with the catalog (G-01 §5.1), and on this screen that request is exactly what failed. Printing a name the interface does not have, or a placeholder where G-01 shows a name, would be an invented value.
- **No back arrow** (map §2.3 rule 1 names G-01 and G-08 as the two screens without one): there is no screen inside the store to go back to.
- **No title in the header.** The message is C02, in the body, where the eye lands.
- No cart bar, no search, no refresh icon, no count or badge beside B03 (§5.6).

So the answer to "do the variants differ in anything besides the button?" is: **yes — in the title and the body line, and in when the reception line appears. The two titles are deliberately different sentences rather than one neutral sentence with a different button, because they describe two different situations for the guest: in one the store exists and is not answering, in the other the thing the guest opened was never the store. Telling both groups "the store could not open" would send the invalid-link guest to tap a Retry that cannot work — which is exactly why that variant has no Retry.

### 5.3 Telling the guest to ask reception (C04) — the decision

- **One string for both variants:** "إذا استمرت المشكلة، اتصل بالاستقبال من هاتف الغرفة" / "If the problem continues, call reception from your room phone". It names the channel (the room phone) because that is the one that works when the store does not, and it is the same channel G-07 §6.2 and G-06 §6.3 name, so the product gives one answer everywhere.
- **In the invalid-link variant it is shown from the first render**, because the guest's own action — scanning again — may well fail the same way, and there is no button to try.
- **In the retryable variant it is hidden on the first render and appears from the first failed Retry onwards**, and then stays for the rest of the visit. Reason: the first failure is very often a phone that lost the hotel Wi-Fi for a moment, and one tap fixes it. Sending every such guest to the telephone at the first stumble would generate calls the hotel does not need; a guest who has now failed twice has a real problem and deserves the number.
- **It is not a link and not a button.** No `tel:` link, no phone number, no "call" icon: the interface does not know the hotel's internal extension, a number printed here would have to come from the store settings that failed to load (§5.1), and the room phone's reception key is one press away from the guest's hand.

### 5.4 "Retry" (B02) — what it does, second by second

1. **On the tap**, B02 switches to the **trying state** synchronously, before the request is assembled: its label is replaced by C05 (spinner and "جارٍ المحاولة… / Trying…"), and B02 becomes non-interactive. There is no instant in which a second tap finds an active button.
2. **One catalog request is sent** — the same request G-01 §6.1 sends on load — with the **same 10-second limit**. The figure is G-01's, restated so that the two screens cannot drift apart.
3. **The trying state lasts a minimum of 800 ms**, even when the request fails sooner (an offline device answers in a few milliseconds). Reason: without a floor, a tap on an offline phone would produce no visible change at all, and the guest would tap harder and faster; 800 ms is long enough to be seen and short enough not to be a wait. The maximum is the 10-second limit.
4. **Success** — a valid catalog arrives within 10 seconds: G-01 replaces G-08 within 100 ms, scrolled to the top, in the language currently shown here (§6.4). A catalog that is valid but contains **zero products** is a success: the guest lands on G-01's empty state (G-01 §6.2), not back here.
5. **Failure** — no connection, a server error, an unreadable response, or 10 seconds elapsed: G-08 re-renders as the retryable variant with B02 active again and C04 now shown. **No error message is added, no counter is shown, and no second message stacks up**; the screen the guest already read is simply still true.
6. **Repeated taps:** a tap during the trying state does nothing. After a failure the button is live again immediately, so the effective ceiling is roughly one attempt per second (the 800 ms floor plus the tap), and on a real timeout one attempt per ten seconds.
7. **No retry limit.** B02 is never disabled permanently, never disappears after N attempts, and no "you have tried too many times" message exists. A guest whose Wi-Fi returns on the eleventh tap must get into the store on the eleventh tap; a cap would strand precisely the guest it claims to protect. C04 is the only thing that changes with repetition.
8. **A response that arrives after its attempt was abandoned** — abandoned by the 10-second limit, or because the guest left G-08 through B03 — is discarded and never navigates the screen: the guest is not moved to G-01 by a request they have stopped waiting for, and a guest reading their orders on G-07 is never pulled out of it by a catalog that answered late.

### 5.5 What happens if the connection returns on its own — the decision

**Nothing happens, by design.** G-08 does not watch the network, does not act on the browser's online/offline events, does not poll in the background, and never reloads or navigates by itself. The screen changes only when the guest taps B02 or B01, or reloads the page.

Reasons: a screen that navigates itself can replace what the guest is reading mid-sentence, and can fire while a finger is travelling toward a button; the browser's "online" signal means an interface is up, not that the hotel's store is reachable, so acting on it would produce a Retry that fails and a screen that flickers; and the guest already has one obvious action whose result is under their control. C03 tells them what it is.

### 5.6 What is deliberately not on this screen

- **No back arrow, no hotel name, no title in the header** (§5.1).
- **No order status, no order count, no badge, no order number, no date, no total, no active-order banner, and no direct link to G-06.** B03 is the only order-related element on this screen and it displays nothing (§5.1, §7.4).
- **No error code, technical detail, status number, or "copy this error" control.** None of it helps a guest in a hotel room, and the hotel's own support does not read it from the guest.
- **No "report this problem", feedback box, e-mail, or chat.**
- **No automatic retry, countdown to the next attempt, attempt counter, or progress bar** (§5.4 rule 5, §5.5).
- **No phone number and no `tel:` link** (§5.3).
- **No illustration, no animation** other than C05's spinner while a Retry runs.
- **No cart bar, no product content of any kind, no store hours notice** (map §4 decision 16).
- **No toast** (G-01 convention). No modal ever opens from G-08.

### 5.7 Why this is a screen and not an error state inside G-01 — restated for the build team

Map §1.3 and §4 decision 13 settled it; this section states it so that nobody merges the two during the build:

1. **It replaces G-01 entirely.** There is no catalog, so there is no hotel name in the header, no "pay on delivery" line, no category bar, no product grid, no floating cart bar, and no active-order banner. An "error state of G-01" would be a G-01 with every one of its own elements missing — which is a different screen wearing G-01's ID.
2. **It is reached from three other screens, not only from G-01.** A failed reload or a direct URL on **G-02** or **G-03** lands here too (map §2.2, G-02 and G-03 rows). A state that lives inside G-01 cannot be shown in place of G-02 or G-03 without G-01 being loaded first — and G-01 is exactly what cannot be loaded.
3. **It carries the language toggle for its own reason** (§5.1): a guest may never reach G-01 at all, and locked decision 7 renders Arabic first.
4. **One of its two variants has no Retry.** An invalid link is not a transient failure of G-01; it is a statement that this URL is not the store. A single "error state" would have to grow a variant system anyway, and it would grow it inside the screen that has the most elements in the product.
5. **G-01's own error section already points here** (G-01 §6.3 case 1: "G-01 is not shown at all; G-08 replaces it… All copy for that case lives in the G-08 file"), and G-01's empty state is a different thing entirely — a catalog that loaded and holds no products (G-01 §6.2), which G-08 must never be used for (§5.4 rule 4).

## 6. States

The whole screen is the product's error surface, so the four template states are defined here against what they mean for **the store's load**, not against a list or a form. Two of the four are rendered.

### 6.1 Loading

**Rendered, in exactly one situation: the trying state of the retryable variant** (§5.4). It is not a separate screen and it does not replace the body: C01, C02, C03 and C04 stay exactly where they are, unchanged, and only B02 changes — its label becomes C05, the spinner and "جارٍ المحاولة… / Trying…", and it stops accepting taps. **B01 and B03 both stay live**: a tap on B01 switches the language without cancelling the attempt, and a tap on B03 leaves for G-07 and abandons it (§5.4 rule 8).

Duration: at least 800 ms, at most 10 seconds (§5.4 rules 2 and 3). It ends in one of exactly two ways: G-01 (§6.4) or the retryable variant again with C04 shown (§6.3).

**There is no loading state on arrival.** The guest reaches G-08 *after* a load already failed somewhere else; G-08 itself fetches nothing in order to draw itself, so it is complete on its first painted frame, offline included.

The invalid-link variant has no loading state at all, because it has no request to make.

### 6.2 Empty

**Unreachable, and deliberately so.** G-08 shows no list, so "empty" has no meaning inside it — and the one case that might be mistaken for it belongs to another screen: a catalog that loads successfully and contains **zero products** is a **success** for G-08's purposes and is rendered as **G-01's empty state** (G-01 §6.2), with its own "تحديث / Refresh" button. G-08 must never be shown for an empty-but-valid catalog; that would tell the guest the store is unavailable when it is open and simply has nothing in it today.

### 6.3 Error

**This is the screen.** "Error" on G-08 is not a message added to something else; it is the rendered state of one of the two variants, and there is never more than one error message on screen at a time.

| Case | What is rendered |
|---|---|
| Arrival for a connection failure, a server error, an unreadable catalog response, or the 10-second timeout | Retryable variant: C01, C02, C03, B02. C04 hidden |
| A Retry attempt that failed (any of the same four causes) | Retryable variant again, identical, with **C04 now shown** and B02 live again. Nothing is appended, nothing turns red, no counter appears (§5.4 rule 5) |
| Arrival for a URL that is not a valid store link | Invalid-link variant: C01, C02, C03, C04. **No B02** |
| A failure that cannot be classified | Retryable variant (§7.1 rule 3) |

The table lists the body blocks only. The header is the same in every case: B01 always, and B03 whenever the device holds at least one readable order record (§5.1) — including in the invalid-link case and after any number of failed Retries.

No modal, no toast, no error code, no stacked messages, and no state in which the guest is left with no readable sentence on screen.

### 6.4 Success

**Never rendered on G-08.** Success on this screen means one thing only: a catalog request returned a valid catalog within 10 seconds, and therefore **the guest is no longer here** — G-01 replaces G-08 within 100 ms of the response (§3.3), scrolled to the top, in the language currently shown, with the device's cart, saved orders and language choice exactly as they were before the failure. There is no "connected again" message, no checkmark, no flash and no transition animation; the store simply opens, which is the only confirmation the guest needs.

A valid catalog containing zero products is a success (§6.2): the guest lands on G-01's empty state.

## 7. Field rules

Nothing is typed on G-08 and no field element exists (§4). The rules below govern the one decision the screen makes (which variant to show), the one request it sends, and the values and sizes it renders.

### 7.1 Which variant is shown — the classifier

Exactly one variant is chosen on arrival, by this rule, in this order:

1. **Invalid link** when the opened URL is not a valid store link. Concretely, either of: (a) the interface's own route rules do not recognise the address as a store address (the store identifier is missing, empty, or malformed); or (b) the server answers the catalog request by stating that no store exists for this address. Nothing else produces this variant.
2. **Retryable** for a failure of the store's own load: no connection when the request is sent, the connection dropping before a response, any server error response, a response that cannot be read as a catalog, or no response within **10 seconds** (G-01 §6.1).
3. **Any failure that cannot be classified is treated as retryable.** There is no third variant and no "unknown" case left to the technical team. The bias is deliberate: offering a Retry to a guest with a broken link costs them one tap and one honest sentence, while withholding it from a guest whose link is fine strands them with no action at all.

A variant is chosen once per arrival and never changes while the screen is shown — except that a failed Retry re-renders the retryable variant with C04 (§6.3), and a Retry whose failure is classified as *invalid link* (case 1b: the server now states that this address is not a store) re-renders the **invalid-link** variant, which then has no Retry.

### 7.2 The catalog request sent by Retry

- One request per accepted tap on B02; never two in flight (§5.4 rule 1).
- Identical to the request G-01 sends on load; **10-second limit**, the same figure as G-01 §6.1.
- Trying state floor **800 ms**, ceiling 10 seconds (§5.4 rule 3).
- The request carries nothing that identifies the guest (locked decision 4).
- A late response is discarded (§5.4 rule 8).
- No limit on the number of attempts (§5.4 rule 7); no back-off, no growing interval, no cooldown beyond the 800 ms floor.

### 7.3 Language

- Rules reused verbatim from G-01 §7.4: Arabic on first open with no saved choice; the saved choice is applied before the first render so no flash of the wrong language occurs; the toggle's label is always the name of the *other* language written in that language; switching re-renders in place with no reload and saves the choice on the device.
- The choice made on G-08 is the choice G-01 opens with when the store loads: the toggle does the same thing here as there, so a guest who switched to English on the failure screen gets an English store.
- A language switch while a Retry is running does not cancel it (§4, B01 row).

### 7.4 Values rendered

G-08 renders **no data of any kind**: no hotel name, no product, no price, no quantity, no order number, no order status, no count, no date, no error code, no phone number. Every string on the screen is fixed interface copy in the two languages, so there is no number to format and the digit, currency and date conventions of G-01 §7.2 have nothing to act on here. This is the only screen in the product that shows no value the server or the device supplied.

**The one exception, stated exactly:** G-08 still renders no *value* supplied by the server or the device. The **existence** of at least one saved order record is the only device fact that reaches this screen, and the only thing it can change is whether B03 is rendered (§5.1). How many records exist, what they hold, and what status they carry are never read and never shown.

### 7.5 Sizes, touch targets, text, and accessibility

- Text sizes: C02 title 20 px bold; C03 16 px; B02 and C05 labels 16 px bold; B01 16 px; **B03 16 px**; C04 14 px. **Minimum text size on G-08 is 14 px.** Nothing on this screen is smaller.
- Tap areas: B01 at least 44 × 44 px; **B03 at least 44 × 44 px**; B02 358 × 56 px. Nothing else on the screen is tappable. B03 sits in the fixed 56 px header, so it changes none of the body-block height arithmetic below.
- The body block S02 is vertically centered under the header; at a 390 × 664 px viewport its tallest form — icon 56 + 24 + title 2 lines 56 + 12 + body 2 lines 44 + 12 + reception line 2 lines 40 + 32 + button 56 = 332 px — sits inside the 608 px available, so no state of G-08 ever scrolls at that size. With enlarged text the body scrolls under the fixed header and B02 remains reachable by scrolling; nothing is truncated.
- Contrast: every text at least 4.5:1 against its background, including the gray icon's stroke. Nothing on the screen is signalled by colour alone — there is no red, no warning colour, and no colour-carried meaning at all; the screen reads correctly in grayscale.
- Browser zoom and text scaling are never disabled (G-01 §7.6).
- Screen reader: on arrival the screen is announced as C02, then C03, then C04 when it is shown, then B02 when it is rendered, then B03 when it is rendered; B01's and B03's accessible names are their visible labels ("English / العربية" and "طلباتي / My orders"). When a Retry starts, "جارٍ المحاولة… / Trying…" is announced once; when it fails, the appearance of C04 is announced once; nothing is announced repeatedly while the spinner turns.
- Motion: the C05 spinner is the only animation on the screen and it does not rotate when the device requests reduced motion (the text alone then carries the trying state). No other element animates, and no transition plays when G-01 replaces G-08.

## 9. Decisions settled on this screen (not previously settled by CLAUDE.md, the map, or an earlier screen)

1. **Exact copy for both variants, in both languages** (§5.2): "تعذّر فتح المتجر / The store could not open" with "تحقّق من اتصال الإنترنت ثم أعد المحاولة / Check your internet connection and try again"; "هذا الرابط لا يفتح متجر الفندق / This link does not open the hotel store" with "امسح رمز QR الموجود في غرفتك مرة أخرى / Scan the QR code in your room again".
2. **One reception string for both variants** — "إذا استمرت المشكلة، اتصل بالاستقبال من هاتف الغرفة / If the problem continues, call reception from your room phone" — always shown in the invalid-link variant, and shown in the retryable variant only from the **first failed Retry** onwards (§5.3). It is text, never a phone number and never a `tel:` link.
3. **The variants differ in the title, the body line, when the reception line appears, and the presence of B02 — and in nothing else** (§5.2): same header, same icon, same layout, same sizes, same toggle, and the same "طلباتي / My orders" control B03 — identical element, identical condition, identical placement on both variants.
4. **Retry runs inside its own button**: B02's label is replaced by the spinner and "جارٍ المحاولة… / Trying…", the button becomes non-interactive on the tap, and the rest of the screen does not change (§5.4, §6.1).
5. **The trying state has a floor of 800 ms and a ceiling of 10 seconds**, the ceiling being G-01's catalog limit restated so the two cannot drift (§5.4, §7.2).
6. **No retry limit, no back-off, no counter, and no failure message that grows with repetition** (§5.4 rule 7, §6.3). The only thing that changes with repetition is the appearance of C04.
7. **A repeated tap cannot fire a second request**; the effective ceiling is about one attempt per second on an offline device (§5.4 rule 6).
8. **The connection returning on its own changes nothing**: no network listeners, no polling, no automatic retry, no self-navigation (§5.5).
9. **Success means leaving the screen**: G-01 within 100 ms, no "connected again" message, no animation; and a valid catalog with zero products counts as success and lands on G-01's empty state, never back here (§6.2, §6.4).
10. **A failure that cannot be classified is retryable** (§7.1 rule 3) — there is no unknown case left undefined.
11. **G-08 has no URL of its own** and leaves the address bar untouched, so it can be shown in place of G-01, G-02 or G-03; B02 always lands on G-01, while a browser reload re-loads whatever URL the guest was on (§3.3).
12. **Arrival has no loading state**; the screen is complete on its first painted frame, offline included (§6.1).
13. **The language toggle is never disabled or hidden, including while a Retry runs**, and the choice made here is the one G-01 opens with (§7.3).
14. **The screen renders no data at all** — the only screen in the product for which that is true (§7.4) — and shows no error code or technical detail (§5.6). **One exception, and only this one:** the *existence* of at least one saved order record is the only device fact that reaches this screen, and the only thing it can change is whether B03 is rendered. No value is ever displayed: no status, no count, no badge, no order number, no date, no total.
15. **Minimum text size 14 px; no colour carries meaning; nothing scrolls at a 390 × 664 px viewport** (§7.5).
16. **Why this is a screen, not a state inside G-01, is written down in §5.7** so the build team does not merge them: it replaces G-01 entirely, it is reached from G-02 and G-03 as well, it carries the language toggle for a guest who may never see G-01, and one of its variants has no Retry.
17. **A guest who lands on G-08 with at least one order saved on the device reaches G-07 through B03, on both variants**, because G-07 needs no catalog and a catalog failure must not consume the cancel-before-acceptance window of locked decision 6 (map §2.2 G-08 row as amended). B03 is rendered only while a readable record exists, it opens the list and never a single order, it is never disabled — including mid-Retry — and it displays nothing about the orders it leads to. (This replaces the flag the draft raised in §5.6 and §9 decision 17, which `/spec/reviews/G-08.md` R1 has now answered.)
