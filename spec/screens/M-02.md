# M-02 — Cancel order? (إلغاء الطلب؟)

Author: Thinker (Agent 1)
Status: **Draft** (status is changed only by the orchestrator in `/spec/screens-map.md`)
Governing documents: `/CLAUDE.md` **locked decision 6** (the guest may cancel only before staff accept — this modal is the act itself), plus locked decisions 4, 5 and 7; `/spec/screens-map.md` §1.2 (M-02 row), §1.3 ("Order already accepted, cannot cancel" is a banner on G-06, not a modal), §2.2 (M-02 row), §2.3 rule 2 (modals are not history entries), §4 decision 11 (the cancellation race) and §4 decision 14 (M-02 **is** dismissable by a backdrop tap, unlike M-01); `/spec/screens/G-06.md` §5.6 (the contract this file honours word for word), §5.5 rule 5 (G-06's polling pauses while M-02 is open) and §9 items 9–12; `/spec/screens/M-03.md` §preamble (the modal conventions this file reuses) and §6.1 (the in-flight pattern); `/spec/screens/M-01.md` §6.1 and §7.2 (the same in-flight pattern and the in-flight flag).

**Conventions reused by reference, not re-decided here.** From G-01: Western digits 0–9 in both languages; every tappable element has a tap area of at least 44 × 44 px (G-01 §7.6); body copy never smaller than 14 px; the five canonical order-status labels of G-01 §5.2; browser zoom and text scaling are never disabled; no toast anywhere. From M-03 §preamble (the file that set the modal conventions for the whole product): a modal is a **bottom sheet**, full viewport width, anchored to the bottom edge, top corners rounded 16 px, light filled, 24 px padding on the sides and top, 16 px at the bottom plus the device's bottom safe-area inset, sliding up over 200 ms on open and removed instantly on close; behind it a **backdrop** in black at 50 % opacity covering the whole screen beneath **including G-06's fixed action bar (G-06-S05)**; the sheet is at most 85 % of the viewport height and its content scrolls inside it if it is taller; buttons are stacked vertically at the bottom of the sheet, full sheet width, **48 px** tall, label 16 px bold, 8 px gap; the primary button is dark filled with light text, the secondary is outlined with dark text; title 18 px bold, body copy 16 px, secondary copy 14 px, all start-aligned in the reading direction; on open, keyboard focus moves to the title and the sheet is announced as a dialog.

M-02 differs from M-03 in exactly two respects, both decided in §5 and listed in §9: **which button is primary** (§5.3) and **the fact that its failure keeps the same two button labels** (§5.5).

---

## 1. ID and name

- **ID:** M-02
- **Name:** إلغاء الطلب؟ / Cancel order?
- **Type:** Modal (bottom sheet) on top of **G-06 Order tracking**. No URL, no history entry (map §2.3 rule 2). It is the only place in the product where an order is cancelled; no screen cancels anything by itself (G-06 §5.6).
- **Language:** the language currently saved on the device (locked decision 7). No language toggle. RTL in Arabic, LTR in English; the order number is laid out left-to-right in both languages.

---

## 2. Purpose

Make the guest confirm, once, that an order still in status New is to be cancelled, send that cancellation, and hand G-06 one of the three outcomes it expects.

---

## 3. Entry and exit points

### 3.1 How the modal opens

| From | Trigger | What M-02 receives | What M-02 shows on opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| G-06 Order tracking | Tap G-06-B02 "إلغاء الطلب / Cancel order", which is rendered only while the last known status is **New** (locked decision 6, G-06 §5.8) | Exactly two values (G-06 §5.6 rule 1): (a) the **order number**; (b) the **current status**, which is always New. Nothing else — no lines, no total, no room number, no record handle | The ready state (§6.0): the sheet slides up over the backdrop with C01, C02, C03, B01 and B02. C04 and C05 hidden. Consecutive-failure count = 0 |

M-02 is never opened from any other screen, never from G-01, G-05 or G-07, never on page load, and never by anything other than a tap on G-06-B02. G-06 ignores further taps on B02 until M-02 is on screen, so a double-tap opens M-02 once (G-06 §7.3).

While M-02 is open, **G-06's status polling is paused** (G-06 §5.5 rule 5), so the status the modal is asking about cannot change on the screen underneath while the question is on the screen.

### 3.2 Where each action leads

| Action on M-02 | Destination |
|---|---|
| Tap "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" (B01) | Stays on M-02 in the in-flight state (§6.1) while one cancellation request is sent. Then, by outcome (§5.4): **cancelled** → M-02 and the backdrop are removed at once and G-06 re-renders as Cancelled (G-06 §5.6); **already accepted** → M-02 and the backdrop are removed at once and G-06 re-renders with the real status and its "already accepted" banner — **no second modal appears** (map §4 decision 11); **failed** → M-02 stays open and re-renders in the failed state (§6.3); G-06 beneath is unchanged |
| Tap "الاحتفاظ بالطلب / Keep order" (B02) | M-02 and the backdrop are removed. G-06 is shown exactly as it was — same status, same scroll position, same record — and its polling resumes with one immediate status request (G-06 §5.5 rule 5). Nothing is sent, nothing is written |
| Tap the backdrop (S01) | Identical to B02 (map §4 decision 14 — M-02 **is** dismissable; only M-01 is not). **Inert while a cancellation is in flight** (§6.1) |
| Browser back gesture or button | Identical to B02 (map §2.3 rule 2). **Ignored while a cancellation is in flight** (§6.1) |
| Hardware "Escape" key, downward swipe on the sheet | Identical to B02, and ignored in flight, exactly like the backdrop |

There is no path from M-02 to any screen other than G-06: no "Back to store", no "My orders", no link, no third button.

### 3.3 Reload, backgrounding, and offline

- **Browser reload while M-02 is open:** modals are not history entries, so the page reloads to **G-06 for the same order number** with no modal (G-06 §3.1, reload row). M-02 holds nothing that needs restoring: it writes nothing to the device (§5.6) and its only memory — the consecutive-failure count — is deliberately not persisted (§7.2). G-06 then renders from the record and sends its own status request, which is what tells the guest whether the cancellation landed. **Accepted limitation, stated so it is not mistaken for an oversight:** if the reload happens after a cancellation actually succeeded on the server but before the response reached this device, the flag "cancelled by the guest on this device" is never written (only a live success writes it, G-06 §5.6), so G-06's C08 will read "ألغى الفندق هذا الطلب / The hotel cancelled this order" instead of "ألغيتَ هذا الطلب / You cancelled this order". The **status** is still correct and no order is left in the wrong state; version 1 accepts the wrong attribution rather than writing a flag for an outcome the device has not yet been told.
- **Tab hidden (backgrounded, device locked, another tab):** M-02 stays open in whatever state it is in. An in-flight request is **not** cancelled and the 15-second timeout is wall-clock (§7.3): returning after more than 15 seconds with no response shows the failed state (§6.3); returning earlier continues the same wait; returning after a response arrived applies that outcome exactly as §3.2 defines it. G-06's polling stays paused throughout (it is paused both by M-02 and by the hidden tab).
- **Device offline when "Yes, cancel" is tapped:** the tap is accepted and the in-flight state is entered anyway — M-02 **never** pre-checks connectivity and never disables or hides B01 because the browser reports itself offline, for the same reason G-06 §5.6 rule 6 keeps B02 alive: the device cannot know the hotel's answer, and blocking the only repair path would strand exactly the guest who needs it. A browser that knows it is offline fails the request immediately, so the failed state (§6.3) can appear in well under a second; the copy there tells the guest to check the connection and try again.

## 5. Content

### 5.1 Sheet layout (S02)

Top to bottom: C01 title → C02 effect line → C03 finality line → (rule + C04 failure block, from the 1st failure) → (C05 reception line, from the 2nd consecutive failure) → B01 "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" → B02 "الاحتفاظ بالطلب / Keep order".

Nothing else is on the sheet: no order lines, no total, no room number, no status label, no timeline, no icon, no illustration, no close "×", no third button, no link (§5.7).

Sheet height at a 390 px viewport, content-driven: about **300 px** in the ready and in-flight states, about **390 px** with C04, about **450 px** with C04 and C05 — all far below the 85 % ceiling (564 px at a 664 px viewport).

### 5.2 The copy, and why the order number is named

- **C01 title — "إلغاء الطلب؟ / Cancel order?"** A question, word for word the modal's name in the map (§1.2), so the two buttons beneath it are its two answers. It never changes: not in flight, not after a failure (§5.5).
- **C02 effect line — "سيُلغى طلبك رقم {order number}، ولن يصل إلى غرفتك." / "Your order {order number} will be cancelled and will not be brought to your room."** This is what cancelling *does*, in the guest's terms: the products stop coming. It is not phrased as a system action ("the order will be set to Cancelled") because the guest is deciding about a delivery, not about a status value.
- **C03 finality line — "لا يمكن التراجع عن الإلغاء، ويمكنك إرسال طلب جديد في أي وقت." / "This cannot be undone. You can place a new order at any time."** Two facts in one line: the act is irreversible (there is no un-cancel anywhere in version 1, G-06 §5.6 rule 7), and the way out is a new order, which is true and reachable — a Cancelled order shows "إعادة الطلب / Reorder" on G-06 (G-06 §5.8).
- **The body names the order number — decision.** C02 carries "{order number}" because the device can hold several active orders at once (G-07 lists them and G-01's banner shows only the most recent, G-01 §5.2), so a guest who arrived at G-06 from a G-07 row must be able to see, on the sheet that is about to do something irreversible, *which* order the sheet means. The number is the one string the guest and the hotel share by phone (G-06 §5.2), so it is also the string C05 asks the guest to quote.
- **The body does not name the room number, the total, the products, or the status.** All four are on G-06 one tap away and unchanged behind the backdrop; repeating them here would turn one question into a second order review, exactly as M-01 §5.4 refuses for the same reason.

### 5.3 Which button is primary — decision

- **B02 "الاحتفاظ بالطلب / Keep order" is the primary button** (dark filled, light text) and sits **last, at the bottom of the sheet, where the thumb rests** after tapping G-06's bottom action bar. **B01 "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" is the secondary button** (outlined) and sits above it.
- **Reason, stated as the rule this file applies:** a mis-tap must fail toward the outcome that can be repaired. Keeping an order that the guest meant to cancel costs one more tap on G-06-B02, which is still on screen and still tappable while the status is New. Cancelling an order that the guest meant to keep cannot be undone (C03) and the guest must place a new order, retype a room number and wait again — and if staff have moved on, it cannot be recovered at all. The destructive answer therefore does not get the dark fill and does not get the resting thumb position; the guest who really means it moves the thumb up and reads a label that contains the word "ألغِ / cancel".
- This is the one place in the product where the primary button is **not** the action the modal is named after. M-01's primary is "تأكيد وإرسال / Confirm and send" and M-03's is "إعادة المحاولة / Retry" because both of those move the guest forward along a path that is recoverable; M-02's forward path is the only irreversible one.
- Both buttons are the same width and the same 48 px height, and neither is a bare text link, so a guest with poor eyesight sees two equal targets (M-01 §5.3 rule). The rank is carried by fill versus outline and by nothing else, so the sheet reads correctly in grayscale.

### 5.4 The request, and the three outcomes

M-02 sends **one** request per tap of B01: a cancellation for this order number. It carries the order number only — nothing that identifies the guest (locked decision 4), no room number, no device identifier, no reason.

The response is classified into exactly the **three outcomes G-06 expects** (G-06 §5.6 rule 2), and never into a fourth:

| What the device observed | Outcome handed to G-06 | What M-02 does |
|---|---|---|
| The server answers that the order is now **Cancelled** | **cancelled** | Closes at once; G-06 writes status Cancelled, the point of cancellation and the "cancelled on this device" flag, and re-renders (G-06 §5.6) |
| The server answers that the order is **not New and not Cancelled** — Accepted & preparing, On the way, or Delivered — and returns that status | **already accepted**, with that status | Closes at once; G-06 writes the real status, hides its "Cancel order" button and shows its "already accepted" banner. **No second modal, no error, no apology sheet** (map §4 decision 11) |
| The request could not be sent, the connection dropped, no response arrived within **15 seconds** (§7.3), the response was an error of any other kind, or the response was unreadable | **failed** | **Stays open** and re-renders in the failed state (§6.3). G-06 is unchanged beneath it and nothing is written (G-06 §5.6 outcome *failed*) |
| The server answers that it holds **no order with this number** | **failed** | Treated as the row above, deliberately: M-02 may not invent a fourth outcome, and "no such order" is already owned by G-06's own status poll (G-06 §5.5 rule 8), which runs again the moment M-02 closes and puts G-06 into its "لم نعثر على هذا الطلب / We could not find this order" error state |
| A response that arrives **after** the 15-second timeout | Nothing is handed over | Discarded (M-01 §3.3 rule): the modal does not change by itself and no outcome is applied. Nothing is written to the device |

**Retrying is safe, and the copy says so.** Cancelling is idempotent: a second cancellation request for an order the server has already cancelled answers *cancelled* again, and one for an order staff have since accepted answers *already accepted*. There is therefore no client key, no duplicate risk, and no reason to lock the guest out after N attempts — which is why C04's line 2 can promise "إعادة المحاولة آمنة: لا يمكن إلغاء الطلب مرتين. / Trying again is safe: an order cannot be cancelled twice."

**The race is resolved only by the server** (G-06 §5.6 rule 4). M-02 does not check the status before sending and does not refuse to send because a poll failed; a guest who taps B01 half a second before staff tap "accept" gets *already accepted*, which is the designed answer, not an error.

### 5.5 The failure case — M-02's alone to hold

The *failed* outcome is the one case G-06 explicitly refuses (G-06 §5.6: "Nothing at all… M-02 owns that case"). Concretely:

1. **The sheet stays open.** The backdrop does not flicker, the sheet does not slide again, and G-06 is never made interactive in between.
2. **C04 appears** above the buttons from the **1st** failed attempt, with its 1 px rule above it, and stays for as long as M-02 is open. Its exact copy is in §4. Line 1 states the two facts the device knows: the cancellation did not reach the hotel, and the order is therefore unchanged. Line 2 states the one action and the one reassurance.
3. **Both buttons remain, with their labels unchanged.** B01 is still "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" and is the retry; B02 is still "الاحتفاظ بالطلب / Keep order" and still closes with nothing changed. **Decision, with its reason:** B01 is not renamed "إعادة المحاولة / Retry" because the button's label must keep saying what the tap will *do* — a neutral "Retry" would hide the irreversible word behind a harmless one, and the guest re-reading the sheet after a failure is exactly the guest who needs to see it. B02 keeps its primary fill, so the safe answer stays the resting one even in the failed state.
4. **C05, the reception line, appears from the 2nd consecutive failed attempt** and then stays. **Threshold decision, with its reason:** one failure is usually a momentary Wi-Fi drop and offering the phone immediately would invite a call that duplicates a tap; but a failing cancellation runs against a clock — staff may accept at any second — so the room phone is offered one failure earlier here than on M-03, which waits for the 3rd (M-03 §5.3). C05 is honest about what reception can do: it asks the guest to quote the order number and it says the hotel may already have started preparing the order, which is the true state of a cancellation that has not landed.
5. **There is one failure copy, not two variants.** M-03 splits connection from server because a guest stuck in a checkout has two different fixes and may be there for minutes; here both causes leave the guest with exactly the same two next steps — tap again, then the room phone — so a second variant would add words without changing behaviour. Checking the connection is harmless advice in the server case.
6. **Nothing else changes with repeated failures:** no attempt counter is displayed, no cap on retries, no automatic retry, no countdown, no change of title, no change of button labels or order, and no new element after C05.
7. **No error is ever shown on G-06 for a failed cancellation** (G-06 §6.3, "Failures that are not this state"), and no toast is shown anywhere.

### 5.6 What M-02 writes to the device — nothing

**M-02 performs no write to the device in any state or outcome.** It does not touch the order record, the cart, the session catalog copy, the saved language, or the order list.

The flag "cancelled by the guest on this device" is written by **G-06**, not by M-02: G-06 §5.0 lists it among the three things G-06 writes, G-06 §5.6 writes it on the *cancelled* outcome together with the status and the point of cancellation, and G-06 §9 item 11 records it as G-06's decision. This file is consistent with that and claims none of it. M-02's entire output is the one outcome value it hands back (§5.4).

Two consequences, both deliberate: the consecutive-failure count lives in memory only and is gone on close or reload (§7.2), and a cancellation whose success this device never learns about is attributed to the hotel on a later visit (§3.3, accepted limitation).

### 5.7 What is deliberately not on this modal

Each item was considered and excluded, because M-02 asks one question and every extra thing on the sheet competes with it.

- **No icon** of any kind, in any state — no warning triangle, no "!" circle, no trash glyph. M-03 opens with an "!" because it reports something the guest did not ask for; M-02 answers a tap the guest just made, and its first line is already a question in 18 px. An icon here would add a shape to interpret without adding a fact.
- **No order summary:** no products, no quantities, no total, no room number, no payment method, no order time. All of them are on G-06 behind the backdrop.
- **No status label and no timeline.** The sheet exists only while the status is New (G-06 §5.8) and repeating "جديد / New" would invite the guest to read the sheet as a status screen.
- **No cancellation-reason field, no reason picker, no "tell us why".** The hotel's operational need is the order not arriving, not the motive; and every field is a keyboard on top of a modal at 2 AM (§4, no F element).
- **No "cancel and reorder", no "edit the order instead", no "change room number" shortcut.** Version 1's repair path is cancel then order again (G-06 §5.11), and it happens on the store, not inside this sheet.
- **No close "×"** in the corner: B02 and the backdrop are the two dismissals, and a third one styled as a neutral glyph would be ambiguous on a sheet whose subject is cancelling.
- **No countdown, no "you have N seconds left to cancel", no attempt counter, no retry cap, no automatic retry.**
- **No phone number or extension for reception** (M-03 §5.6 rule: hotel room phones reach reception with a labeled key, and an extension would need a store setting that does not exist in version 1).
- **No language toggle, no hotel name, no toast, before, during or after.**

## 6. States

M-02 has exactly **three rendered states — ready, in flight, and failed** — and two template states that are not rendered inside it, with the rule that makes each so.

### 6.0 Ready (the state on open)

On screen: S01 backdrop; S02 sheet with C01, C02, C03, B01 (active), B02 (active); C04 and C05 hidden. Consecutive-failure count 0. No keyboard. Screen-reader focus is on C01 and the sheet is announced as a dialog (M-03 convention).

Both buttons ignore taps for the first **300 ms** after the sheet starts to appear (the arming delay of M-01 §7.2, reused here because this sheet slides up under a thumb that has just tapped a button in the same screen region). After 300 ms both are live.

### 6.1 Loading — reachable: the cancellation in flight

Triggered by: a tap on B01 after the arming delay. Entered **synchronously on the tap**, before the request is assembled or sent, by setting an in-flight flag (M-01 §7.2 rule 2), so there is no instant in which a second tap can find B01 still active.

On screen, for the whole in-flight period:
- C01, C02, C03 unchanged, and C04/C05 unchanged if a previous failure already showed them (the guest keeps reading what they were reading — M-03 §6.1 rule).
- B01 keeps its outlined shape and size at 100 % opacity; its label is replaced by the 20 px spinner and "جارٍ الإلغاء… / Cancelling…"; it is inert, and a tap on it produces no visual reaction.
- B02 at 50 % opacity, inert.
- S01 backdrop inert; browser back, Escape and a downward swipe on the sheet are ignored.
- No other spinner, progress bar, dimming, countdown or elapsed-time text.

Rules:
- The in-flight period ends at the first of (a) a response, (b) the **15-second** timeout (§7.3). It never lasts longer than 15 seconds of wall-clock time, including across a backgrounded tab (§3.3).
- **A second tap is impossible**, by the flag above: one tap equals exactly one request.
- **The modal cannot be dismissed while in flight** — decision: if the guest could close it mid-flight, an *already accepted* answer arriving a second later would have nowhere to go, and G-06 would keep showing "Cancel order" for an order the hotel had already taken. The wait is capped at 15 seconds instead, after which the failed state offers the way forward.
- A screen reader announces "جارٍ الإلغاء… / Cancelling…" once when the state is entered.

### 6.2 Empty — not reachable

M-02 has no list and no data that could be absent. Its only variable content is `{order number}`, which is always present: G-06 renders B02 only for an order it holds a readable record for (G-06 §6.2, §6.3) and hands the number over on the tap (G-06 §5.6 rule 1). If a build ever reached M-02 without an order number, that is a G-06 defect, not an M-02 state, and M-02 must not open.

### 6.3 Error — reachable, and it is M-02's alone: "failed"

Triggered by: the *failed* outcome of §5.4 (request not sent, connection dropped, 15-second timeout, error response, unreadable response, or "no such order").

On screen: the same sheet, re-rendered in place with **no second slide-up and no backdrop flicker** — C01, C02, C03 unchanged; **C04** added above the buttons with its rule; **C05** added under C04 from the 2nd consecutive failure; B01 back to its rest label "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" and active; B02 back to full opacity and active. The consecutive-failure count + 1 (§7.2).

Nothing on G-06 changes: no banner, no status change, no record write, no error text (G-06 §5.6, outcome *failed*).

A screen reader announces C04's two lines once when they first appear, and C05 once when it first appears; neither is re-announced on later failures.

There is **no field error message on M-02** — nothing is typed (§7).

### 6.4 Success — not rendered inside M-02

Neither of the two successful outcomes draws anything inside the sheet:

- ***cancelled*:** M-02 and the backdrop are removed at once, with no exit animation, no checkmark, no "تم الإلغاء / Cancelled" flash and no toast, within 100 ms of the response. G-06 is the screen that reports it: its status headline reads "ملغى / Cancelled", its timeline shows New → Cancelled, and its C08 reads "ألغيتَ هذا الطلب / You cancelled this order" (G-06 §5.6). That report is G-06's copy, not this file's.
- ***already accepted*:** M-02 and the backdrop are removed at once, in the same way and in the same 100 ms, and **no second modal opens** (map §4 decision 11). G-06 shows the real status and its "already accepted" banner, whose two lines G-06 §4 owns word for word. M-02 shows no apology, no explanation and no "too late" message of its own, because the guest would then have to dismiss a second sheet before being able to read the answer that is already on the screen behind it.

In both cases G-06's polling resumes when M-02 closes, with one immediate request (G-06 §5.5 rule 5).

## 7. Field rules

**Nothing is typed on this modal and no `M-02-F` element exists** (§4). Concretely: no keyboard opens while M-02 is shown, no value is entered, edited or validated, there is no character limit to enforce, no accepted format to check, and **no field error message exists on M-02 in any state**. The only error copy on the sheet is C04/C05, which reports a failed request, not a bad value (§5.5).

The rules below govern the values M-02 was handed, the single request it sends, and the taps it accepts.

### 7.1 `{order number}` — handed over, never re-checked

- Source: G-06, which read it from the device order record (G-06 §5.6 rule 1).
- Printed exactly as handed: Western digits 0–9 in both languages, no "#", no thousands separator, no padding, no truncation — identical to the number on G-05, G-06 and G-07 (G-06 §7.1). Laid out left-to-right in both languages.
- M-02 never validates it, never reformats it, and never changes it. It appears in C02 at 16 px and in C05 at 14 px, as plain text in both places; a tap on either does nothing, and the browser's phone-number detection is turned off for C05 so no dial link is ever rendered.

### 7.2 Consecutive-failure count

- Whole number. **0** when M-02 opens; **+ 1** on every *failed* outcome; never decreased.
- Drives exactly two things: C04 is shown at ≥ 1, C05 at ≥ 2.
- **Never displayed** to the guest, in any form.
- **Memory only, never written to the device** (§5.6). It is discarded when M-02 closes by any route — B02, the backdrop, browser back, a successful cancellation, an *already accepted* answer, or a page reload — so reopening M-02 always starts at 0 and shows the ready state.
- No upper bound: after ten consecutive failures B01 is still "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" and still tappable.

### 7.3 Timeout — 15 seconds, and why it matches the submission

- **One attempt has 15 seconds**, measured wall-clock from the moment the request is sent; at 15 seconds with no response the attempt is *failed* (§5.4) and the failed state is drawn (§6.3).
- **This is the same figure M-01 and M-03 use for the submission** (M-03 §preamble, §7.3) and deliberately **not** G-06's 10 seconds. The reason is the shape of the wait, not the request: G-06's 10 seconds belongs to a background poll that repeats every 20 seconds, so cutting an attempt short costs nothing there — the next one is seconds away. M-02's request is a one-off the guest is watching with the screen blocked, exactly like a submission, and cutting it at 10 seconds would abandon a cancellation that might still be landing and push the guest into a retry that only adds another 15 seconds. One number for every "the guest is waiting on a modal" request also means one number for the technical team to configure.
- There is **no cancel-the-request control** and browser back does not abort the request (§3.2, §6.1); the wait is capped instead. A response arriving after the cap is discarded (§5.4).

### 7.4 Tap handling

- B01 and B02 each respond to a single tap, after the 300 ms arming delay (§6.0).
- After a tap on B01 the in-flight flag makes every tap anywhere on M-02 — B01, B02, the backdrop, the sheet padding — do nothing until the outcome arrives (§6.1), so one tap equals exactly one request and a double-tap cancels once.
- After a tap on B02, or on the backdrop, M-02 ignores every further tap until it is removed, so a double-tap closes once and cannot also reach G-06's action bar underneath.
- Nothing else on M-02 is tappable: not the title, not the body lines, not C04, not C05, not the order number. A tap on any of them does nothing at all — no navigation, no selection, no visual reaction.
- There is no long-press, no swipe gesture other than the dismissing downward swipe on the sheet (§3.2), and no pull-to-refresh.

### 7.5 Language

- Every fixed string renders in the language saved on the device at render time (locked decision 7). There is no toggle on M-02; switching happens on G-01 and G-08 only (map §2.3 rule 6).
- The order number is never translated and never reformatted by a language switch.
- The quotation marks around the button name inside C04's line 2 follow the language: «…» in Arabic, “…” in English, exactly as written in §4.

### 7.6 Sizes, touch targets, text, and accessibility

- B01 and B02: full sheet width (342 px at a 390 px viewport) × 48 px, with an 8 px gap; the backdrop is the whole remaining screen. Every tappable element therefore exceeds 44 × 44 px (G-01 §7.6).
- Text sizes: C01 18 px bold; C02, C03 and C04 16 px (C04 line 1 bold); C05 14 px; button labels 16 px bold. **Nothing on M-02 is smaller than 14 px.**
- Contrast: every text at least 4.5:1 against its background, including the disabled B02 label at 50 % opacity and the spinner. Rank is carried by fill versus outline and never by colour alone, so the sheet reads correctly in grayscale.
- Browser zoom and text scaling are never disabled; under scaling the sheet grows up to 85 % of the viewport height and then scrolls inside, with both buttons reachable.
- Screen reader: on open, focus moves to C01, the sheet is announced as a dialog, and C01, C02 and C03 are read in order with the order-number digits read **one at a time** ("one zero four two", the M-01 §5.2 convention), then the two buttons in their layout order — "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" first, "الاحتفاظ بالطلب / Keep order" second. Entering the in-flight state announces "جارٍ الإلغاء… / Cancelling…" once. C04 and C05 are each announced once when they first appear. While M-02 is open, G-06's content behind the backdrop is not reachable by the screen reader's focus order.
- Motion: the 200 ms slide-up on open and the rotating 20 px spinner are the only movement; there is no exit animation and no other animation in any state.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

Each statement is pass/fail.

1. M-02 opens only from a tap on G-06-B02 "إلغاء الطلب / Cancel order", which exists only while the last known status is New; there is no other way to reach M-02 in the product.
2. M-02 receives exactly two values from G-06 — the order number and the current status — and sends no request on opening.
3. On open, the sheet shows the title "إلغاء الطلب؟ / Cancel order?" (18 px bold), C02 "سيُلغى طلبك رقم {order number}، ولن يصل إلى غرفتك. / Your order {order number} will be cancelled and will not be brought to your room.", C03 "لا يمكن التراجع عن الإلغاء، ويمكنك إرسال طلب جديد في أي وقت. / This cannot be undone. You can place a new order at any time.", and the two buttons; C04 and C05 are not rendered.
4. C02 prints the order number exactly as G-06 shows it, in Western digits in both languages, laid out left-to-right.
5. "الاحتفاظ بالطلب / Keep order" is the dark-filled primary button and is the **lower** of the two; "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" is outlined and sits above it; both are 48 px tall and span the sheet width.
6. Both buttons ignore taps for the first 300 ms after the sheet starts to appear, and respond normally after that.
7. Tapping "الاحتفاظ بالطلب / Keep order" closes M-02 with no request sent and no write to the device; G-06 is shown with the same status, the same scroll position and the same record, and sends one status request immediately.
8. Tapping the backdrop, using the browser back gesture, pressing Escape, or swiping the sheet down while no cancellation is in flight does exactly what "الاحتفاظ بالطلب / Keep order" does.
9. Tapping "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" sends exactly one cancellation request carrying the order number only, and within 100 ms replaces B01's label with a 20 px spinner and "جارٍ الإلغاء… / Cancelling…" at 100 % opacity while B02 goes to 50 % opacity.
10. While the cancellation is in flight, a tap on B01, a tap on B02, a tap on the backdrop, browser back, Escape and a downward swipe all do nothing, and exactly one request exists for that tap.
11. The in-flight state ends within 15 seconds in every case; with no response by then the sheet re-renders in the failed state, and a response arriving after that changes nothing and writes nothing.
12. When the server answers that the order is Cancelled, M-02 and the backdrop are removed at once with no checkmark, no message and no toast, and G-06 shows the order as Cancelled per its own rules.
13. When the server answers with a status that is neither New nor Cancelled, M-02 and the backdrop are removed at once, G-06 shows that real status with its "already accepted" banner, and **no second modal of any kind appears**.
14. When the request fails — not sent, dropped, timed out, error response, unreadable response, or "no order with this number" — M-02 stays open, re-renders in place with no second slide-up, and nothing on G-06 changes: no banner, no status change, no record write, no error text.
15. On the 1st failed attempt the sheet shows C04 with exactly the two ruled lines in §4, separated from C03 by a 1 px rule; on the 2nd consecutive failed attempt it also shows C05 with exactly its ruled copy and the order number; both stay for as long as M-02 is open.
16. After a failure both buttons are present with unchanged labels and unchanged order, B01 is active and tappable, and tapping it sends the identical request again; after ten consecutive failures B01 still reads "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" and is still tappable.
17. No attempt counter, countdown, automatic retry, retry cap, or change of title is ever shown on M-02.
18. The reception line C05 is plain text: tapping it does nothing and the browser does not render the order number or anything else in it as a phone link.
19. M-02 writes nothing to the device in any state or outcome; the "cancelled by the guest on this device" flag is written by G-06 on the *cancelled* outcome, and the consecutive-failure count exists only in memory and is 0 again whenever M-02 is reopened.
20. Reloading the browser while M-02 is open lands on G-06 for the same order number with no modal, and M-02 is not restored.
21. Backgrounding the tab during the in-flight state does not cancel the request; returning after more than 15 seconds with no response shows the failed state, returning earlier continues the same wait, and returning after a response applies that outcome.
22. With the device offline, tapping "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" still enters the in-flight state and then shows the failed state; B01 is never hidden or disabled because the device is offline.
23. G-06's status polling is paused for the whole time M-02 is open and resumes with one immediate request when M-02 closes by any route.
24. No order summary, room number, total, product list, status label, timeline, icon, close "×", third button, link, language toggle or hotel name appears on M-02 in any state.
25. No field exists on M-02, no keyboard opens while it is shown, and no field error message is displayed in any state.
26. Nothing on M-02 is tappable except B01, B02 and the backdrop; taps on the title, the body lines, C04, C05 and the order number do nothing.
27. Nothing on M-02 is smaller than 14 px; every text has a contrast of at least 4.5:1; the sheet reads correctly in grayscale, with rank carried by fill versus outline and never by colour alone.
28. Pinch-zoom and browser text scaling work while M-02 is open; with large text the sheet stops growing at 85 % of the viewport height and scrolls inside, with both buttons reachable.
29. A screen reader announces the sheet as a dialog, reads C01, C02 and C03 with the order-number digits one at a time, then the two buttons in layout order; it announces "جارٍ الإلغاء… / Cancelling…" once on entering the in-flight state and C04 and C05 once each when they first appear.
30. No toast is shown at any point while M-02 opens, waits, fails, or closes.

## 9. Decisions settled on this screen (not previously settled by CLAUDE.md, the map, or an earlier screen)

1. **"الاحتفاظ بالطلب / Keep order" is the primary button and sits last, at the thumb's resting position; "نعم، ألغِ الطلب / Yes, cancel" is the outlined secondary above it** (§5.3). A mis-tap must fail toward the outcome that can be repaired, and only one of the two outcomes can be undone. This is the one modal in the product whose primary button is not the action it is named after.
2. **The body names the order number** (§5.2): the device can hold several active orders, and the sheet that is about to do something irreversible must say which order it means — and the number is the string C05 then asks the guest to quote to reception.
3. **The body carries exactly two sentences**: what cancelling does (the products will not come) and that it cannot be undone, with the true way out (place a new order). No room number, no total, no product list, no status label (§5.2, §5.7).
4. **No icon anywhere on M-02, in any state** (§5.7): M-03's "!" announces something the guest did not ask for; M-02 answers a tap the guest just made.
5. **The in-flight pattern is M-01's and M-03's, not a third one** (§6.1): entered synchronously on the tap by an in-flight flag; the tapped button keeps its shape at 100 % opacity with a 20 px spinner and "جارٍ الإلغاء… / Cancelling…"; the other button at 50 % opacity; backdrop, browser back, Escape and swipe all inert. One tap equals exactly one request, and a second tap is impossible.
6. **M-02 cannot be dismissed while a cancellation is in flight**, even though it is freely dismissable otherwise (map §4 decision 14): an *already accepted* answer arriving after an early close would have nowhere to go, and G-06 would keep offering to cancel an order the hotel had already taken (§6.1).
7. **Timeout 15 seconds per attempt, matching M-01 and M-03 and deliberately not G-06's 10 seconds** (§7.3): a one-off request the guest watches with the screen blocked is capped like a submission, not like a poll that repeats every 20 seconds.
8. **The failed state keeps the same two buttons with the same labels; B01 is the retry** (§5.5 rule 3). Renaming it "إعادة المحاولة / Retry" would hide the irreversible word behind a neutral one, and the safe answer keeps its primary fill even in the failed state.
9. **One failure copy, not two variants** (§5.5 rule 5): unlike M-03, both causes leave the guest with the same two next steps, so a second variant would add words without changing behaviour.
10. **The reception line appears from the 2nd consecutive failure — one failure earlier than M-03's 3rd** (§5.5 rule 4): a failing cancellation runs against staff acceptance, so the one path that does not depend on the connection is offered sooner; and it says plainly that the hotel may already have started preparing the order.
11. **Retrying a cancellation is safe and the copy says so** (§5.4): cancellation is idempotent, so there is no client key, no duplicate risk, no retry cap, and no automatic retry.
12. **"No order with this number" is handed back as *failed*, not as a fourth outcome** (§5.4): G-06's own status poll already owns that case and runs the moment M-02 closes.
13. **A response arriving after the 15-second cap is discarded and writes nothing** (§5.4), unlike G-06's late poll response, because M-02 writes nothing at all.
14. **M-02 writes nothing to the device, ever** (§5.6). The "cancelled by the guest on this device" flag is **G-06's** write, exactly as G-06 §5.0, §5.6 and §9 item 11 already record; this file claims none of it. Accepted, stated consequence: a reload inside the 15-second window of a cancellation that did succeed leaves the order correctly Cancelled but attributed to the hotel (§3.3).
15. **Neither successful outcome draws anything inside the sheet** (§6.4): on *cancelled* and on *already accepted* alike, M-02 and its backdrop are removed within 100 ms and G-06 does all the reporting — no success flash, no apology sheet, and above all **no second modal** (map §4 decision 11).
16. **No reason field and no reason picker** (§5.7, §7): the hotel's need is that the order stops, not the motive, and a field here would open a keyboard on top of a modal at 2 AM.
17. **The 300 ms arming delay of M-01 applies to both buttons** (§6.0), because this sheet slides up under a thumb that has just tapped a button in the same screen region.
18. **Three rendered states — ready, in flight, failed** — with empty unreachable and success never drawn inside the modal (§6).
