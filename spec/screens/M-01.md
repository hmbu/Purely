# M-01 — Confirm room number (تأكيد رقم الغرفة)

Author: Thinker (Agent 1)
Status: **Draft** (status is changed only by the orchestrator in `/spec/screens-map.md`)
Governing documents: `/CLAUDE.md` locked decision 2 (this modal exists because of it) and decisions 1, 3, 4, 7; `/spec/screens-map.md` §2.2 (M-01 row), §2.3 rules 2 and 3, §4 decisions 6, 10 and 14; `/spec/screens/G-04.md` §3.1, §7.7 and §7.8 (what M-01 receives and what G-04 does when M-01 closes); conventions set by `/spec/screens/G-01.md` (Western digits 0–9 in both languages; tap area of at least 44 × 44 px for every tappable element; browser zoom and text scaling never disabled; no toast anywhere in version 1).

This modal is the only guard between a mistyped room number and a delivery to the wrong door. G-04 §5.3 decided that the room number is **not** checked against a list of real rooms in version 1, so nothing on the device or the server will catch "305" typed as "350". M-01 catches it by making the guest read the number once, large, with nothing else competing for attention, and by making sure the order is sent exactly once when they confirm. Every rule below serves one of those two duties.

---

## 1. ID and name

- **ID:** M-01
- **Name:** تأكيد رقم الغرفة / Confirm room number
- **Type:** Modal. Opens on top of G-04 Checkout and has no URL and no history entry (map §2.3 rule 2). It is **non-dismissable** (map §4 decision 14): the only two ways out are its two buttons.
- **Language:** the language currently saved on the device (locked decision 7). M-01 has no language toggle. The layout is RTL in Arabic and LTR in English; the room number itself is always laid out left-to-right, digit by digit, in the order typed, in both languages (a digit string has no reading direction to flip).

---

## 2. Purpose

Show the typed room number in large type, alone, and send the order exactly once only after the guest confirms it.

## 3. Entry and exit points

### 3.1 Entry (what opens M-01)

| From | Trigger | What M-01 receives | What M-01 shows on open |
|---|---|---|---|
| G-04 Checkout | Tap on G-04-B05 "إرسال الطلب / Submit order" after every rule in G-04 §7.1–§7.4 passed (G-04 §7.6 rule 6) | The handoff in G-04 §7.8, read-only: (1) the trimmed room number string, e.g. "0305"; (2) the order payload — cart lines (product identifier, quantity, unit price from the session catalog copy), trimmed notes or empty, payment method "card" / "cash", amount (whole number) or none, total, interface language, and the client order key | The **ready state** (§6.0): backdrop over G-04, the card centered, the room number in large type, both buttons active. There is no other entry. M-03 and M-04 do **not** reopen M-01: after a failed submission the guest returns to G-04 (M-03 "Close") and taps "Submit order" again, which opens M-01 afresh from G-04 with the draft's current key (G-04 §7.7, acceptance criterion 42). A browser reload never restores M-01 (§3.3).

**Snapshot rule.** M-01 takes the handoff once, at open, and keeps it unchanged until it closes. What it displays and what it sends are byte-for-byte the values handed at open. G-04 is covered by the backdrop, so nothing on this tab can change the cart or the draft while M-01 is open; M-01 does not re-read the cart or the draft at any later moment.

### 3.3 Reload, backgrounding, and device sleep

- **Reload while M-01 is open (any state):** the page reloads on G-04 per G-04 §3.1 "browser reload" row — summary rebuilt, draft restored including the client order key, M-01 not shown (modals are not history entries). If a request was in flight at the moment of reload, its outcome is unknown to the device; the order is **not** saved on the device (that happens only on a received success response, §7.4). The guest taps "Submit order" again; because nothing changed, G-04 hands the **same key** (G-04 acceptance criterion 42), and the server treats a request with a key it has already fulfilled as that same order (map §4 decision 10), so a reload mid-request cannot create a duplicate. This is the one situation in which the guest re-confirms an order that may already exist; the outcome is one order, shown on G-05.
- **Tab backgrounded, device locked, or screen turned off while the request is in flight:** M-01 stays in the loading state. The 15-second timeout (§7.3) is measured in **wall-clock time from the moment the request was sent**, not by a timer that pauses in the background. When the tab returns to the foreground: if a response arrived meanwhile, it is handled by the outcome rules as if it had just arrived; if no response arrived and 15 seconds or more have elapsed, the wait is abandoned and M-03 opens (Outcome 2, kind `timeout`); if fewer than 15 seconds have elapsed, the wait continues until the 15-second mark.
- **Page discarded by the browser while in the background** (memory pressure) and reloaded on return: identical to "reload while M-01 is open".
- **A response arriving after M-01 has already left the loading state** (late success after a timeout, for instance) is **discarded** and never applied to the screen. The duplicate-safe key in M-03's "Retry" is what brings such an order back to the guest.

## 5. Content

### 5.1 Card placement and size (S01 / S02)

- The backdrop S01 covers everything, including G-04's fixed bottom bar, so G-04-B05 cannot be tapped while M-01 is open (G-04 §4, B05 row).
- The card S02 is 358 px wide and **vertically centered** in the viewport. It is a centered dialog, not a bottom sheet, for two reasons: a bottom sheet signals "swipe down to dismiss", which this modal does not allow; and a centered card keeps its buttons away from the bottom 88 px of the viewport, where G-04-B05 was a moment ago (§5.3, §7.2).
- **Clearance rule:** the card's bottom edge is never closer than **96 px** to the bottom of the viewport. If the viewport is too short for a centered card to keep that clearance (a landscape phone, or a very short viewport), the card is positioned with its bottom edge exactly 96 px above the viewport bottom and its content scrolls inside the card; the two buttons are always the last content and are always reachable by scrolling inside the card.
- The card has no close "×", no drag handle, and no title-bar icon.
- Horizontal padding 24 px; the usable inner width is 310 px.

### 5.2 The room number (C01, C02, C03)

- C01 title "تأكيد رقم الغرفة / Confirm room number", 18 px bold — the modal's name from the map, so the tester, reviewer and wireframer all call it one thing.
- C02 "سيُوصَّل طلبك إلى الغرفة / Your order will be delivered to room", 16 px. The label states the consequence of confirming, so a bare number cannot be mistaken for an order number, a total, or a quantity. It is a statement, not a question, and the two button labels are the answers.
- C03 the number: **64 px, bold**, letter-spacing 0.08 em, centered in a 112 px tall bordered box that spans the card's inner width. It is the largest thing on the screen by design — the next largest text on M-01 is 18 px, so the number is at least 3.5 times the size of anything else — and no other element on M-01 uses a size above 18 px. The letter-spacing keeps "0305" from reading as one blob at this size and makes a leading zero visibly its own digit. Five digits at 64 px with this spacing measure about 230 px, which fits the 310 px inner width with margin; the number never wraps or shrinks.
- The value is the trimmed string handed by G-04, shown **exactly**: "0305" is shown as "0305", never "305" (G-04 §7.1 "leading zeros"). Western digits 0–9 in both languages (G-01 convention). No thousands separator, no "Room" prefix inside the box, no "#".
- Contrast: near-black digits on the light-gray box, at least **7:1**; every other text on M-01 is at least 4.5:1 against its background. Nothing on M-01 is distinguished by color alone: the number is set apart by size, weight, and the box; the primary button by its dark fill; the disabled B02 by 50 % opacity. The wireframe is grayscale and the built screen must read correctly in grayscale.
- The digits are read by a screen reader **one digit at a time** ("zero three zero five"), never as a quantity ("three hundred five"), so that a guest listening to the modal hears the leading zero.

### 5.3 The two buttons (B02, B01)

- Order, top to bottom: **B02 "تعديل رقم الغرفة / Edit room number" (outlined), then B01 "تأكيد وإرسال / Confirm and send" (dark filled, primary)**. Both are full width and 56 px tall; both labels 16 px bold. The primary is the last element, where the thumb rests after G-04's bottom bar; the escape hatch sits between the number and the primary, so the eye passes "Edit" before reaching "Confirm".
- B01 is the only visually primary element. B02 is never styled as primary and never as a bare text link (a low-vision guest must see two equal-sized targets).
- Neither button contains the room number, the total, or any icon.
- No third action exists: no "Cancel", no "×", no "Back to cart". A guest who wants to change anything other than the room number taps B02, which lands on G-04 where "Edit cart" and the back arrow are one tap away.

### 5.4 What is deliberately not on this modal

Each item below was considered and excluded because M-01 has one job (locked decision 2) and every extra line competes with the number for the guest's attention.

- **No order total, no payment method, no item count, no notes, no line list.** The guest saw all of these on G-04 one tap ago and G-04 already forces an explicit payment choice with no default (G-04 §5.5); repeating them here turns a room-number check into a second order review and pushes the number up the card and out of the guest's eye line.
- No order number (it does not exist until the server responds; G-05 shows it).
- No "I have checked the number" checkbox and no countdown before B01 becomes active beyond the 300 ms arming delay (§7.2); reading friction that can be clicked through without reading is not protection.
- No inline editing of the number (§4, "no field element").
- No progress bar, elapsed-time counter, or "taking longer than usual" text during sending (progress cues during a wait are already deferred in `/spec/backlog.md` G-01-L2; the timeout in §7.3 caps the wait).
- No "Cancel sending" button (§7.3, rule 6).
- No error text of any kind (§6.3): every failure leaves M-01 for M-03 or M-04.
- No success message, checkmark, or "Sent!" flash (§6.4): G-05 is the success screen (map §4 decision 6).
- No toast (G-01 convention). No language toggle. No hotel name.

## 6. States

M-01 has exactly two rendered states: **ready** and **loading**. The other two template states are unreachable, with the rule that makes them so.

### 6.0 Ready (the state on open)

On screen: S01 backdrop; S02 card with C01, C02, C03, B02 (active), B01 (active); C04 hidden. Both buttons ignore taps for the first 300 ms after the card starts to appear (§7.2); after that both are live. The keyboard is closed. Screen-reader focus is inside the card (§7.6).

### 6.1 Loading (request in flight)

Triggered by: a tap on B01 (after the arming delay). Entered **synchronously on the tap**, before the request is assembled or sent, so there is no instant in which a second tap can find B01 still active.

On screen:
- S01, S02, C01, C02, and C03 unchanged: the room number stays visible at 64 px for the whole wait.
- B01 keeps its dark pill shape and size; its label is replaced by C04 — the 20 px spinner and "جارٍ الإرسال… / Sending…". B01 is non-interactive: every tap on it is ignored and produces no visual reaction.
- B02 is disabled: 50 % opacity, taps ignored. Reason: editing the room number while a request whose outcome is unknown is in flight could leave one order on the server and a differently addressed one in the guest's hands; the wait is capped at 15 seconds, after which M-03 offers the way forward.
- Backdrop taps, browser back, Escape and swipe are ignored exactly as in the ready state.
- No text changes anywhere else; no progress indication beyond C04.

Duration: from the tap until the first of (a) a response, (b) the 15-second timeout (§7.3). It never lasts longer than 15 seconds of wall-clock time (§3.3). A screen reader announces "جارٍ الإرسال… / Sending…" once when the state is entered.

Exit: one of the three outcomes in §3.2; M-01 never returns from loading to ready. If the guest must try again, that happens from M-03 (same key) or from G-04 (M-01 reopened afresh).

### 6.2 Empty

**Unreachable.** M-01 opens only from G-04 after §7.1 of G-04 has passed, which guarantees a room number of 2–5 digits, and G-04's render guard (G-04 §3.1) guarantees a cart with at least one line and no out-of-stock line. There is no rendering of M-01 with a blank number box or an empty payload; if a build ever reaches M-01 with an empty room number or zero lines, that is a G-04 defect, not an M-01 state, and M-01 must not open (G-04 stays as it is with B05 doing nothing for that tap).

### 6.3 Error

**Not rendered inside M-01.** Every failure outcome closes M-01 and opens M-03 (connection, timeout, server, or unrecognized response) or M-04 (stock rejection) in its place in the same frame (§3.2, §7.5). M-01 therefore contains no error text, no error element, and no retry control; the copy for those cases lives in the M-03 and M-04 files. The reason is the one-job rule: an error message under the room number would invite the guest to fix the wrong thing here.

### 6.4 Success

**Not rendered inside M-01.** A success response performs the steps in §7.4 and then G-05 is the screen the guest sees; M-01 shows no checkmark and no message of its own. The transition from the loading state to G-05 happens within 100 ms of the response and G-04 is dropped from the history at the same time (map §2.3 rule 3), so browser back from G-05 goes to G-01 and never re-shows G-04 or M-01.

## 7. Field rules

Nothing is typed on M-01 and no field element exists (§4). The rules below govern the values M-01 was handed, the two taps it accepts, the single request it sends, and the response it applies.

### 7.1 Values handed by G-04 — no re-validation

- M-01 **does not re-run** any rule from G-04 §7.1–§7.4 on the room number, notes, payment method, or amount. G-04 is the single owner of the field rules and its §7.8 guarantees that everything handed over has passed them. Two validators for one value drift apart and produce the "which screen is right?" question this spec exists to prevent.
- M-01 displays the room number byte-for-byte (§5.2) and sends the payload byte-for-byte (§7.3). It does not trim, reformat, strip leading zeros, recompute the total, or re-sort the lines.
- M-01 does not read the cart, the draft, or the session catalog copy at any point; it holds the snapshot taken at open (§3.1) and nothing else.

### 7.2 Arming delay on open

- For **300 ms** after the card begins to appear, B01 and B02 ignore taps. Reason: G-04-B05 sits in the bottom 88 px of the viewport and M-01 opens within 100 ms of a tap on it; a guest's second tap of a double-tap (G-04's own 300 ms debounce absorbs it on B05, but the tap lands on whatever is on screen at that instant) must never confirm an order that has not been read. The clearance rule in §5.1 keeps M-01's buttons out of that region as well; the two rules together mean a double-tap on "Submit order" can only ever land on the backdrop, which does nothing.
- The delay is not shown to the guest: the buttons look active from the first frame; a tap inside the 300 ms simply has no effect. There is no countdown and no disabled styling for it.

### 7.3 Sending exactly once — how a second submission is prevented

The order is sent **once per tap on B01**, and B01 can be tapped effectively **once per M-01 instance**. Five layers, from the tap outward:

1. **Lock on the tap, before the request.** The first accepted tap on B01 switches M-01 to the loading state synchronously (§6.1): B01 becomes non-interactive and B02 disabled before the payload is even assembled. No window exists between the tap and the lock.
2. **In-flight flag.** M-01 holds a "request in flight" flag set on that tap and never cleared while M-01 is open; while it is set, every tap anywhere on M-01 is ignored. M-01 never returns to the ready state (§6.1 "Exit"), so a second tap has no state to act on.
3. **One request per instance.** An M-01 instance sends at most one request in its lifetime. Retrying lives in M-03 (map §2.2 M-03 row), and a fresh attempt from G-04 is a fresh M-01 instance.
4. **Client order key (map §4 decision 10; G-04 §7.7).** The request carries the key exactly as handed. M-01 never creates, changes, or discards the key. The key was created the first time M-01 opened in this checkout session and is replaced by G-04 whenever any order value changes afterwards (a cart line or quantity, the room number, notes, payment method, or amount). Consequences M-01 relies on:
   - **Same order, sent again** (M-03 "Retry"; or M-03 "Close" → G-04 unchanged → "Submit order" → new M-01 → "Confirm and send"; or a reload mid-request → same path): same key, so a request whose response was lost is recognized by the server as the same order and returns that order rather than creating a second one. M-01 treats that reply as a success (§7.4) exactly like a first-time success.
   - **Changed order** (B02 → the guest edits the number → "Submit order" → new M-01): new key, so the changed order is a new order and can never be swallowed as a "duplicate" of an attempt that was never confirmed.
   - The key is discarded together with the draft on success (G-04 §7.7), so an order that reached G-05 can never be re-sent under its own key.
5. **Server rule for the technical team (behavior, not backend design):** a submission whose client order key has already produced an order must return that existing order as a success, with its order number, and must not create another. Without this rule layer 4 protects nothing.

Additional rules of the request:

6. **No cancel while in flight.** There is no "Cancel sending" control and browser back does not abort the request (§3.2). Abandoning a request whose server-side outcome is unknown is exactly the ambiguity the key exists to remove; the wait is capped instead.
7. **Timeout: 15 seconds** of wall-clock time from the moment the request is sent (§3.3). If no response has arrived by then, M-01 stops waiting and opens M-03 with kind `timeout`, keeping the payload and key untouched. The figure is longer than G-01's 10-second catalog timeout because this is the one request that must not be given up on lightly, and short enough that a guest on failing hotel Wi-Fi is not left staring at a spinner; because the retry in M-03 is duplicate-safe, ending the wait early costs nothing but a tap.
8. **Payload sent:** the handoff of G-04 §7.8 unchanged — lines (product identifier, quantity, unit price), notes, payment method, amount or none, total, interface language, client order key — plus the room number string. Nothing is added by M-01 (no device identifier, no name, no location, locked decision 4).

### 7.4 Success response — what M-01 does, in order

A success response is one that contains the server's order number for this submission (whether newly created or matched by key, §7.3 layer 4). M-01 then performs the following steps **in this order, all before G-05 is shown**, so that a crash or a closed tab between steps can never leave an order that the device does not know about:

1. **Save the order record on the device** (map §2.3 rule 5; locked decision 4). The record contains: the server order number; the order time (the server's timestamp if the response carries one, otherwise the device time at receipt); the room number string as sent; the lines, each with product identifier, product name in **both** languages as held in the session catalog copy at open, quantity, and unit price; the notes; the payment method; the amount or none; the total; the status **New** (G-01 §5.2 canonical labels). Names are stored in both languages so G-06 and G-07 can render the order after a language switch and after a product leaves the catalog.
2. **Empty the cart** on the device (map §2.3 rule 4: a successful submission is one of the two things that empty it).
3. **Discard the checkout draft and the client order key** (G-04 §7.7).
4. **Replace G-04 in the history** so that back from G-05 goes to G-01 (map §2.3 rule 3).
5. **Close M-01 and show G-05**, handing it the order record from step 1. G-05's own file decides what it shows; M-01 guarantees only that the record exists on the device before G-05 is drawn.

Steps 1–5 complete within 100 ms of the response. The session catalog copy and its stock marks are not touched.

### 7.5 Failure classification — three outcomes, no fourth

Every possible result of the request falls into exactly one bucket; there is no "unknown" bucket left to the technical team:

| Result | Bucket | M-01 hands over |
|---|---|---|
| Response with an order number (new or matched by key) | **Success → G-05** (§7.4) | The saved order record |
| Response that identifies one or more lines as unavailable (out of stock, or no longer in the catalog — the server reports both the same way) | **Stock rejection → M-04** | The list of unavailable product identifiers; the identical payload |
| No connection when sending; connection lost before a response; no response within 15 s; any server error response; any response that is neither of the two above (malformed, unexpected) | **Failure → M-03** | The identical payload; the identical client order key; the kind: `no-connection` (the request could not be sent or the connection dropped), `timeout` (15 s elapsed), `server-error` (everything else) |

M-01 does not decide the copy M-03 or M-04 show; it only hands them the items in the last column. In all three buckets M-01 closes in the same frame in which the next modal or screen appears, so G-04 is never interactive in between.

### 7.6 Sizes, touch targets, text, and accessibility

- Text sizes: C03 room number **64 px bold**; C01 18 px bold; C02 16 px; B01, B02, C04 labels 16 px bold. **Minimum text size on M-01 is 16 px** — larger than the 12 px floor of G-04 and the 10 px floor of G-01 — because this modal exists for a guest who must read one thing correctly.
- The room number is at least 3.5 × the size of any other text on M-01, and nothing else on M-01 exceeds 18 px. This is the concrete meaning of "large type" in locked decision 2.
- Tap areas: B01 and B02 are 310 × 56 px, above the 44 × 44 px convention. They are separated by a 12 px gap so a thumb on one cannot brush the other.
- Contrast: number at least 7:1; all other text at least 4.5:1; disabled B02 at 50 % opacity, like G-01's disabled "+". Nothing is signaled by color alone (§5.2).
- Browser zoom and text scaling are never disabled on M-01 (G-01 §7.6, G-04 §7.9). If the guest enlarges the browser's text, the card grows with its content and scrolls inside per the clearance rule (§5.1); the number never shrinks below 64 px.
- Screen reader: M-01 is announced as a dialog; on open, focus moves into the card and the announced text is C01, then C02, then the digits of C03 one by one (§5.2). Focus is trapped inside the card while M-01 is open; the focus order is C01 → C02 → C03 → B02 → B01. When the loading state is entered, "جارٍ الإرسال… / Sending…" is announced once. G-04 beneath is hidden from assistive technology while M-01 is open.
- Motion: the 200 ms open fade and the C04 spinner rotation are skipped when the device requests reduced motion; the state changes are then instantaneous, with no other difference.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

Each statement is pass/fail.

1. Tapping G-04-B05 with every G-04 field valid opens M-01 within 100 ms on top of G-04: the backdrop S01 covers the entire viewport including G-04's bottom bar, and the card S02 is vertically centered and 358 px wide at a 390 px viewport.
2. M-01 shows, top to bottom: C01 "تأكيد رقم الغرفة / Confirm room number", C02 "سيُوصَّل طلبك إلى الغرفة / Your order will be delivered to room", C03 the room number in its bordered box, B02 "تعديل رقم الغرفة / Edit room number", B01 "تأكيد وإرسال / Confirm and send"; C04 is not rendered.
3. C03 renders the room number at 64 px bold in Western digits; no other text on M-01 is larger than 18 px; the number is laid out left-to-right in both the Arabic and the English interface.
4. Typing "0305" on G-04 and opening M-01 shows exactly "0305" in C03, with the leading zero; typing "12345" shows "12345" on one line without wrapping or shrinking.
5. The interface language on M-01 is the one saved on the device; M-01 has no language toggle; all copy is Arabic in the Arabic interface and English in the English interface.
6. Tapping anywhere on the backdrop S01, in the ready or loading state, changes nothing: M-01 stays open, no message or animation appears.
7. The browser back gesture or button while M-01 is open, in either state, leaves M-01 open in the same state and G-04 unchanged beneath; an in-flight request is not cancelled. Escape on a hardware keyboard and a downward swipe on the card behave the same way.
8. Tapping B02 in the ready state closes M-01 on the next frame and returns to G-04 with F01 focused, the numeric keypad open, and the cursor at the end of the value; no request is sent; the draft and the client order key are unchanged.
9. A tap on B01 or B02 within 300 ms of M-01 starting to open has no effect; a tap after 300 ms has its normal effect. The buttons are not shown in a disabled style during those 300 ms.
10. A double-tap on G-04-B05 (both taps within 300 ms) opens M-01 once and never sends a request: the second tap lands either on B05 during its own debounce or on M-01's backdrop, because the card's bottom edge is at least 96 px above the viewport bottom.
11. On a viewport too short to center the card with 96 px of clearance beneath it, the card's bottom edge sits exactly 96 px above the viewport bottom and the card's content scrolls inside it, with B02 and B01 reachable by scrolling.
12. Tapping B01 (after the arming delay) switches M-01 to the loading state on the same tap: B01 shows C04 (spinner and "جارٍ الإرسال… / Sending…") in place of its label, B02 is at 50 % opacity, C01, C02 and C03 are unchanged, and exactly one request is sent.
13. In the loading state, ten rapid taps on B01, on B02, and on the backdrop send no additional request and change nothing on screen.
14. The request payload is byte-for-byte the handoff of G-04 §7.8 plus the room number string: lines with product identifier, quantity and unit price; notes; payment method; amount or none; total; interface language; client order key. M-01 adds nothing and alters nothing.
15. The client order key in the request is the one handed by G-04; M-01 never generates or changes a key.
16. When the server responds with an order number, M-01, in this order and within 100 ms: writes the order record (order number, time, room number, lines with names in both languages, quantity and unit price, notes, payment method, amount, total, status New) to the device; empties the cart; discards the draft and the key; replaces G-04 in the history; closes and shows G-05 with that record.
17. After criterion 16, browser back from G-05 goes to G-01, and neither G-04 nor M-01 is shown again for that order.
18. If the server responds that the key has already produced an order, M-01 behaves exactly as in criterion 16 with that existing order's number, and no second order exists on the server.
19. When the response identifies unavailable lines, M-01 closes and M-04 opens in its place in the same frame with the list of unavailable product identifiers and the identical payload; the cart, draft and key on the device are unchanged; G-04 is not interactive between them.
20. When the request cannot be sent, the connection drops, or the server returns an error or an unrecognized response, M-01 closes and M-03 opens in its place in the same frame with the identical payload, the identical client order key, and the failure kind (`no-connection` or `server-error`); the cart, draft and key on the device are unchanged.
21. When no response has arrived 15 seconds (wall-clock) after the request was sent, M-01 closes and M-03 opens with kind `timeout`; the loading state never lasts longer than 15 seconds.
22. A response that arrives after M-01 has left the loading state is discarded: nothing on screen changes and no order record is written by it.
23. Backgrounding the tab or locking the device during the loading state, then returning after a response arrived, applies that response as in criteria 16, 19 or 20; returning after 15 seconds with no response opens M-03 with kind `timeout`; returning earlier continues the wait to the 15-second mark.
24. Reloading the browser while M-01 is open (either state) shows G-04 with the draft restored and M-01 closed; tapping "Submit order" again with nothing changed opens a new M-01 carrying the same client order key, and confirming it results in exactly one order on the server.

26. No error text, retry control, checkmark, success message, or toast is ever rendered inside M-01.
27. M-01 never shows the order total, payment method, item count, notes, or order number.
28. In the ready state, no element on M-01 is disabled and no element of G-04 can receive a tap.
29. M-01 never shows a rendering with an empty number box or a payload of zero lines; if G-04 would hand either, M-01 does not open.
30. B01 and B02 are each 310 × 56 px with a 12 px gap; C01 is 18 px bold; C02 is 16 px; button labels and C04 are 16 px bold; nothing on M-01 is smaller than 16 px.
31. The room number's contrast against its box is at least 7:1 and every other text's contrast is at least 4.5:1; the modal is fully legible when rendered in grayscale.
32. A screen reader announces M-01 as a dialog on open, reads C01, C02, and then the digits of C03 one at a time (a leading zero is read as "zero"), keeps focus inside the card, and announces "جارٍ الإرسال… / Sending…" once when the loading state is entered.
