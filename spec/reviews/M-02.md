## 3. The Critical finding, ruled in full

### M2-R1 — F-A: "no order with this number" is folded into *failed* and answered with copy that can never succeed. **Essential — and the fix removes a state instead of adding one.**

**Reason:** for that one server answer, M-02's failure copy points the guest at the single action guaranteed to fail again, forever, while the true answer — the order is not in the hotel's system, call reception — is one dismiss the guest has no reason to make.

**Ruling — M-02 does not render this case; it closes and hands it to G-06.** M-02's own file already reasons that G-06 owns "no such order" (§5.4, decision 12); the error was not the reasoning but the routing. So:

1. **A fourth outcome, not a fourth rendered state.** M-02's outcome set becomes **cancelled / already accepted / order not found / failed**. Its rendered states stay exactly **three** — ready, in flight, failed (§6). `order not found` is drawn nowhere inside the sheet, exactly like the two successful outcomes (§6.4), so M-02 gains no copy, no element, no variant and no second purpose.
2. **How M-02 recognises it — stated narrowly, and biased toward *failed*.** Only a response that was **received and read** and that **states, in the response body the device could parse, that the hotel holds no order with this number** is classified as `order not found` — the same answer G-06's own status poll classifies under G-06 §5.5 rule 8. Everything else stays *failed*: a request that could not be sent, a dropped connection, the 15-second timeout, any other error response, an unreadable or ambiguous body, and a response of any kind arriving after the 15-second cap (which is discarded and hands nothing back, §5.4 last row, unchanged). If the device cannot tell the two apart, it is *failed* — the classification that keeps the sheet open and retryable.
3. **What M-02 does on it.** M-02 and its backdrop are removed at once, within the same 100 ms and with no exit animation, no message, no checkmark and no toast, exactly as on *cancelled* and *already accepted* (§6.4). **What it passes back:** the outcome value `order not found`, for the order number G-06 handed it on opening. Nothing else — no status (there is none), no copy, no error object. M-02 still writes nothing to the device (§5.6, decision 14 unchanged).
4. **What G-06 does with it — its existing state, unchanged in wording.** G-06 enters the error state it already specifies in §6.3: C15's three ruled lines ("لم نعثر على هذا الطلب / We could not find this order", "قد يكون أُرسل من جهاز آخر… / It may have been sent from another device…", "للسؤال عنه، اتصل بالاستقبال من هاتف الغرفة / To ask about it, call reception from your room phone") with an action bar containing only B04. G-06 enters it **directly on receiving the outcome**, not by waiting for its resumed poll to rediscover it: polling does not resume, because polling stops in the error state (G-06 §5.5 rule 8, §6.3). The device record is **not** deleted (G-07 §5.4 owns the only deletion), and no new copy is written anywhere.
5. **No new failure copy is written for this case, on either screen.** G-06's existing three lines are the answer, and they already carry the reception guidance that M-02's C04 could not honestly give.
6. **This is a change to the G-06 contract and G-06's file must accept it.** G-06 today states, in §5.6 rule 2 and in §9 item 9, that it expects "exactly one of three outcomes, with no fourth". That sentence becomes four outcomes, with `order not found` mapped to the error state of §6.3 as a fourth trigger. The exact edits are listed in §7.1 item 1. Until G-06 is revised, the two files disagree; they must be revised in the same cycle.

**What this ruling deliberately does not do:** it does not add a fourth rendered state to M-02, does not write a second failure variant (decision 9 stands), does not let M-02 show reception guidance of its own beyond C05's existing threshold rule, and does not give M-02 any knowledge of G-06's copy.

### 4.4 The three findings the product manager asked to be ruled with their reasoning stated

**F5 — does a modal racing a deadline differ from the other two waits? No.** The 15-second cap and the in-flight lock are **Essential and unchanged**: M-02 §6.1 gives the reason this review endorses — a modal closable mid-flight leaves an *already accepted* answer with nowhere to land, and G-06 would keep offering to cancel an order the hotel had already taken. The progress cue is **Later**, joining G-01-L2, G-03-L7, M-01-L3 and M-03-L2. The race does not change the ruling, for three reasons: a cue removes no seconds from the request; the only device-side action that could remove seconds — aborting early and re-sending — would put a second cancellation on the wire while the first may still be landing and would reopen the dismissal hole above; and losing the race is not a broken journey but a designed outcome (*already accepted* plus G-06's banner, map §4 decision 11), with the room phone offered one failure earlier here than on M-03 precisely because of the clock (decision 10, upheld). The auto-retry half of the finding is rejected outright (decision 11 stands): an automatic retry against an idempotent endpoint adds requests without changing the server's answer, and it would fire while the guest is reading C04.

**F7 — does F4's wording already answer the guest's real need? Yes, for the part version 1 can answer.** The persona's stated fear is the *cost* of cancelling — retyping the room number, re-choosing payment, re-adding every product. M2-R3's sentence removes the largest part of that cost from the unknown: the items come back in one tap. What remains — that the guest must still confirm a room number and a payment method on a fresh checkout — is the documented shape of version 1's repair path (map §3, G-06 §5.11) and is not M-02's to soften. No control, no partial-edit affordance and no "cancel and reorder" shortcut is added (decision 3, §5.7 unchanged).

**F6 — the accepted limitation stays accepted.** It is already written in the file as a limitation rather than an oversight (§3.3), the write belongs to G-06 (§5.6, G-06 §9 item 11), and the residual harm is one mis-worded attribution line on a Cancelled order whose status is correct. Version 1 accepts it.
