## 3. Rulings on the findings that change the screen

### R1 — G-06-01 (Critical, personas 4 and 7): once the status passes New there is no repair and no guidance toward one. **Essential.** And G-06-06 (framing): nothing says cancelling repairs any mistake. **Essential.**

**Reason:** "Cancel order" correctly disappears the moment staff accept (locked decision 6), but nothing replaces it, so a guest who notices a wrong room number or a wrong item at exactly that moment is told nothing at all. That is the moment a wrong delivery proceeds silently, which is the operational-error bar. The second half is the same defect one step earlier: M-01, G-05 and G-06 all advertise cancellation as the remedy for a wrong *room number*, so the guest with a wrong *item* never realises the same path is theirs. Both are cured by copy.

**Ruling — one persistent line of copy, not machinery. New element `G-06-C16`, with exactly two status-dependent variants.**

| Property | Decision |
|---|---|
| ID / type | **G-06-C16** — content block, one persistent line of copy. Not a control: it is not tappable, has no link, no icon, no close control, no fill and no border. It never fades, never auto-dismisses, and is never a toast (G-01 convention). |
| Size | 14 px regular, start-aligned, contrast at least 4.5:1, wraps to a maximum of **3 lines** in the 358 px inner width. |
| Placement | In the body, **directly under C06 (the room-number line) and directly above the timeline S03**: 8 px below C06, 16 px above S03. It therefore sits exactly where G-05 puts C05 — under the room number — so the repair sentence is in the same place on both screens. The layout order of §4 becomes: … C05 → C06 → **C16** → S03 … |
| Variant A — status **New** | Arabic: `هل هناك خطأ في هذا الطلب — رقم الغرفة أو المنتجات أو طريقة الدفع؟ ألغِ الطلب ما دام الفندق لم يقبله بعد، ثم أرسل طلبًا جديدًا.` <br> English: `Something wrong in this order — the room number, the items, or the payment method? Cancel it while the hotel has not accepted it yet, then place a new order.` |
| Variant B — status **Accepted & preparing** or **On the way** | Arabic: `لم يعد بالإمكان إلغاء هذا الطلب من هاتفك بعد أن قبله الفندق. إذا كان فيه أي خطأ — رقم الغرفة أو المنتجات — اتصل بالاستقبال من هاتف الغرفة الآن.` <br> English: `This order can no longer be cancelled from your phone now that the hotel has accepted it. If anything in it is wrong — the room number or the items — call reception from your room phone now.` |
| Shown on | Exactly three statuses: **New** (variant A), **Accepted & preparing** and **On the way** (variant B). |
| Hidden on | **Delivered** (nothing can be repaired and nothing is coming), **Cancelled** (the order is already stopped and B03 "Reorder" is the forward action), and in the **error state** (§6.3, where C15 already names the room phone). |
| Also suppressed when | **C02 is shown.** C02 says the same two facts for the specific case of a lost cancellation race and, as amended by R2, carries the same instruction; showing both would be the same sentence twice in one view. C16 returns if C02 is cleared by leaving the screen and coming back while the status is still Accepted & preparing or On the way. |
| Transition | When a status response moves the order from New to Accepted & preparing, C16 swaps from variant A to variant B in the same 100 ms repaint as C04, the timeline, C06's tense and the action bar (§5.5 rule 6). No animation, no scroll change, no announcement beyond the existing status announcement. |

**Explicitly rejected for version 1 — none of these may appear in the revision:**

1. A **contact button** of any kind ("Call reception", "Contact staff", "Report a problem").
2. A **`tel:` link** — the room phone is dialled from the room phone, and a tappable number invites a call from a mobile that may not reach the hotel's internal extension. C16 is an instruction, exactly like C02's and C15's existing lines.
3. A **chat channel** or any message-to-staff field (G-06 has no field element and gains none).
4. An **edit-order path**: no "change the room number", no "add an item", no "change payment method".
5. A **modal** for any of this. The Critical finding is answered by a line, not by a dialog.
6. **Any reopening of cancellation after acceptance** — no "request cancellation", no "ask the hotel to stop it", no grace period. **Locked decision 6 stands, unchanged.** C16 variant B is a statement that the window is closed, not a way around it.

**Why a line and not a button:** the product's own rule (§5.10) is that the hotel's staffed channel is the room phone. A button cannot dial it, and any on-screen channel the hotel does not staff would be worse than silence. The whole cost of this ruling is one line of copy and one element ID.

**§5.10 must be corrected with it:** the sentence "the only two places this product ever mentions reception are C02, C15 and G-07's empty state" (which already miscounts) becomes four places, naming C16, and the "no contact staff, chat, call button" exclusion is kept verbatim — C16 is copy, not a control, and the exclusion is what keeps it that way.

### R2 — G-06-11: C02's phone line is ambiguous about whether calling can still change anything. **Essential, scoped to wording, riding with R1.**

**Reason:** C02 appears at the single most time-pressured moment on this screen — the guest has just tried and failed to cancel — and "to ask about it" tells them a phone call is for information after the fact, so a guest whose order is going to the wrong room may not make the call at all. One line, and it must agree with C16 variant B rather than contradict it.

**Ruling:** C02's **line 2** copy is replaced; line 1, the two-line structure, the sizes, the absence of a close control and the lifetime rule (§5.6 rule 5) are all unchanged.

- Arabic: `إذا كان في الطلب أي خطأ، اتصل بالاستقبال من هاتف الغرفة الآن.`
- English: `If anything in the order is wrong, call reception from your room phone now.`

It promises no outcome (the interface cannot know whether the hotel will act) and it is not a link. It is the same instruction as C16 variant B, which is why C16 is suppressed while C02 is shown (R1).

### R3 — G-06-03: the §5.9 fit proof omits C05 and is therefore not complete on its own terms. **Essential.**

**Reason:** §5.9 offers arithmetic "rather than hope" as the guarantee for the one safety requirement this screen carries, and the arithmetic omits an element the same file places between C04 and C06 and allows to appear while the status is still New — exactly the weak-connection case. A proof that does not hold on its own terms is a defect in a spec meant to be built from without questions, whether or not the real slack absorbs the gap. R1 adds a second omitted element, so the proof must be redone once, completely.

**Ruling — §5.9 is rewritten with every element that can be on screen in status New, and with the safe-area inset the file states elsewhere.**

1. **Basis:** viewport 390 × 664 px; **the action bar's safe-area inset counted explicitly at 34 px** (§4's S05 row already requires "12 px padding plus the device's safe-area inset", so the New-status bar occupies **148 + 34 = 182 px**); C05 shown at its worst case of 2 lines; C06 at its worst case of 2 lines; C16 variant A at its worst case of 3 lines; C02 not shown (it cannot appear while the status is New).
2. **The redone sum, from the top of the viewport:** 56 S01 + 24 top padding + 24 C03 + 8 + 28 C04 + 8 + **40 C05 (2 lines)** + 8 + 68 C06 (2 lines) + 8 + **60 C16 (3 lines)** = **332 px** to the bottom of C16.
3. **The bar's top edge** is at 664 − 182 = **482 px**. Free space between C16 and the bar: **150 px**, enough for the first two timeline rows (56 px each).
4. **The guarantee therefore still holds, and now holds on its own terms:** at 390 × 664 px with a 34 px safe-area inset, default text size, C05 shown and C16 at 3 lines, **C03, C04, C05, C06, C16 and B02 are all fully visible on the first painted frame without scrolling.**
5. **What must give if a future change breaks the inequality**, ruled now so it is never decided ad hoc: the sacrifice order is **(1) the timeline rows scroll out of the first screenful** — they are information, not a repair — then **(2) C05 may wrap below the fold**. **Never** C06, **never** C16, **never** B02: the room number, the repair sentence and the cancel control share the first screenful in status New, or the requirement of `/spec/reviews/G-04.md` R3 is not met. No element may be shrunk, collapsed, or moved into an expandable region to make the sum work, and C06 never renders below 28 px.
6. **The past-New case is proved too, in one line:** with C02 shown (≈ 80 px including its padding and the 16 px below it), C16 suppressed, and the bar at 80 + 34 = 114 px, the bottom of C06 sits at 344 px against a bar top of 550 px — C02, C04 and C06 are all on the first screenful.
7. §5.9's closing paragraph about enlarged text stays: the body scrolls, S05 stays fixed, B02 never leaves the viewport, and the room number is above the cancel control in the scroll order.
8. **Acceptance criterion 6** is rewritten to name C05, C16 and the 34 px inset; one criterion is added for the past-New case in item 6.

### 4.4 The two rulings the product manager left to the reviewer, stated deliberately

**G-06-04 and G-06-05 — status staleness. Later.** I deferred the equivalent on G-01 as backlog **G-01-L4**, and the tester has raised it again here and on G-07. G-06 is genuinely different: it is the live screen and the guest is watching it, so this was decided on its own merits rather than by precedent. The deferral stands, for three reasons stated here so the decision is on the record:

1. **The only decision a guest makes from the status on this screen is whether to tap B02**, and §5.6 rule 4 already rules that the race is resolved by the server, never by the screen: a guest who taps "Cancel order" against a 30-second-old "New" gets the *already accepted* outcome with C02 (now amended by R2), which is the designed answer, not an error. A stale label therefore cannot produce a wrong outcome — only a wrong expectation for at most 20 seconds.
2. **The screen self-corrects on a fixed, stated cadence** (20 s, 10 s timeout), and a definite failure already speaks through C05. What the finding asks for is confidence about the gap between two correct states.
3. **The cheap candidates are all worse than silence here:** a "last updated 14:12" line is a second number for the guest to interpret next to a status that may be perfectly current; a refresh control is a second way to do what the screen already does every 20 seconds (§5.5 rule 9); a spinner every 20 seconds is movement without information on a screen ruled to have no animation at all.

Deferred together as one backlog row **G-06-L1**, so the escalation half (G-06-05) is not lost separately.

**G-06-10 — silent hotel-initiated cancellation. Later.** Push notification is not considered at all: it is impossible without a login and would breach locked decision 4, as the product manager states. The question is only whether anything cheap is owed on screen, and the answer for version 1 is no:

1. **Nothing on G-06 can speak while G-06 is closed.** The finding's real surface is **G-01's active-order banner**, which hides identically for Delivered and Cancelled (G-01 §5.2). G-01 is **Approved**; changing its banner to a fourth state that survives the order's end is a new visibility rule, a new lifetime rule and a new dismissal rule — not a one-line change, and not G-06's to make.
2. **The guest is not misinformed, only uninformed.** Both discovery paths already state the truth in full: "طلباتي / My orders" (G-07) lists the order with its status, and one tap lands on G-06 where C04, the timeline and C08 say who cancelled it and why.
3. **A hotel that cancels a room-service order normally telephones the room**, which is outside this interface and is the channel C02, C15 and now C16 all point at.

Deferred as backlog row **G-06-L3**, written so the version-2 work is named precisely: distinguishing Delivered from Cancelled on G-01's banner until the guest has opened the order once.

## 6. The three required checks
